Steps 1 and 2 of domain/mltt: the informal argument (written before the
.seg files; Consistency.seg and Typing.seg follow it step by step).

Notation.  Types.seg: s <= t (Le s t) means s has at least the information
of t, so the paper's information order u [= v is v <= u.  s == t is
s <= t and t <= s.  Components, and pi/arrow selections PSel r s t,
ASel r d t (the meets s, t, resp. d, t of the parts of chosen pi,
resp. arrow, components; the empty selection gives om, om) are those of
Types.seg.  md r is the depth of r, meets not counted; the parts of a
selection of r have depth <= md r - 1.

1. Consistency

The paper's compact elements are elements of a CUSL: two of them may have
no join.  A type is a finite meet of components; it is CONSISTENT when the
components have a join in C.  For a predicate P on types ("consistent"
for the parts), the clauses CL P r are

  (c1) r has no univ component together with a pi component;
  (c2) for a univ component and an arrow selection d, t of r:
         P d  implies  om <= t;
  (c3) the same for a pi component;
  (c4) for every pi selection s, t of r: P s and P t;
  (c5) for every arrow selection d, t of r: P d implies P t.

(c2), (c3): U and Pi(a,g) are in other summands than lambda; they are
compatible with lambda(f) only when lambda(f) = bot, i.e. when every value
of f at a consistent argument is om-like.  (c5) is "keys compatible =>
values compatible" for all the entries at once (a selection is any finite
set of entries).  P occurs negatively in (c2), (c3), (c5), so consistency
is not an inductive definition; it is defined by recursion on a natural
number N (fuel):

  C 0 r = true,      C (N+1) r = CL (C N) r,      Cons r = C (md r) r.

Lemma 1.1 (enough fuel).  If md r <= a and md r <= b then
C a r <=> C b r.
Proof.  Induction on a and b.  (0, 0): trivial.  (N+1, M+1): the clauses
compare C N and C M at parts s of r, md s <= md r - 1 <= N, M: induction.
(0, M+1): md r = 0, so r has no pi or arrow component (a component has
depth <= md r); (c1), (c3) are vacuous, an arrow selection of r is empty
up to meets, so its codomain is om-only and om <= t (c2); the parts in
(c4), (c5) have depth 0, where C M holds by induction (C 0 = true).
(N+1, 0): C 0 = true.

Lemma 1.2 (fixpoint).  Cons r <=> CL Cons r.
Proof.  Cons r = C (md r) r <=> C (md r + 1) r = CL (C (md r)) r by 1.1,
and at a part s, C (md r) s <=> C (md s) s = Cons s by 1.1 (md s <= md r);
the clauses are invariant when P is replaced by an equivalent predicate on
the parts.

After 1.2 the definition is not opened again.

Lemma 1.3 (down).  r <= k and Cons r imply Cons k.  (A type with less
information than a consistent type is consistent; so Cons respects ==.)
Proof.  First for every fuel N: C N r and r <=s k imply C N k, by
induction on N, each clause of k being transported from r:
 - a univ (pi) component of k is reached from r <= k by a univ (pi)
   component of r (Types.sel-down, les-inv);
 - an arrow selection d, t of k gives one d', t' of r with d <=s d' and
   t' <=s t (Types.asel-tr); C N d gives C N d' by induction, then (c5)
   or (c2)/(c3) for r, and back to t by induction (resp. om <= t' <= t);
 - pi selections the same way (Types.psel-tr).
Then with N = max (md r) (md k) and 1.1 at both ends.

Lemma 1.4.  Cons om, Cons univ (depth 0); Cons A and Cons phi give
Cons (pi A phi) (by 1.2: the clauses of a single pi component: no univ,
arrow selections om-only, pi selections s, t with A <= s, phi <= t, and
1.3).

2. Semantic typing (paper Fig. 2, in the intensional form of Prop. 2.15)

One inductive family Tp indexed by two judgement forms:
  el u a       "u : a"
  ent f A phi  "the entries of f are typed over A, phi": each arrow
               component x -> v of f has x : A and v : y for some y with
               phi <= x -> y  (y is phi(x) up to more information; the
               application phi(x) is never computed).

  Bot   om <= u, a : U                              =>  u : a
  Univ  u == U, a == U                              =>  u : a
  Pi    u == Pi(A, phi), a == U, A : U,
        ent phi A (om -> U), Cons phi               =>  u : a
  Lam   u == f, a == Pi(A, phi), Pi(A, phi) : U,
        ent f A phi, Cons f                         =>  u : a
  ent:  ent om A phi;
        x : A, phi <= x -> y, v : y                 =>  ent (x -> v) A phi;
        ent f1 A phi, ent f2 A phi                  =>  ent (f1 /\ f2) A phi.

Every rule concludes u : a for any u, a in the classes of the canonical
forms, so typing is invariant under == (Lemma 2.1) without a conversion
rule, and the rules are syntax-directed on the shape of the class
(generation, Lemma 2.3).  The family of a Pi is a function of type
Pi(A, _ |-> U), i.e. its entries are typed over A and the constant family
om -> U.  The representation f in Lam is existential: the paper's remark
that the properties hold only for a well-chosen representation.  In the
paper Prop. 2.15 is a theorem relating dom(f, a) and "for all u,
f(u) : g(u)" to this form; here the intensional form is the definition,
as in the three formalisations of the paper (section 4), and what is used
of the extensional form is Lemma 2.8 below.  There are no stages, so the
paper's stability (Prop. 2.18) has no counterpart.

Lemma 2.1 (conversion).  u : a, u == u', a == a' give u' : a'.
Proof.  Compose the == premises; Bot by induction on its premise a : U.

Lemma 2.2 (presupposition).  u : a gives a : U.
Proof.  Bot: premise.  Univ, Pi: a == U.  Lam: Pi(A, phi) : U and 2.1.

Lemma 2.3 (generation).  A derivation of u : a ends with one of the four
rules (inversion of the family at the form el).
Consequences, with no confusion (Types: om, U, Pi distinct):
 (a) a' : U and a' <= U give a' == U;
 (b) a' : U and a' <= Pi(A, phi) give a' == Pi(A1, phi1) with A1 <= A,
     phi1 <= phi, A1 : U, ent phi1 A1 (om -> U), Cons phi1
     (Pi injectivity of <=: Pi(A1,phi1) <= Pi(A,phi) iff A1 <= A and
     phi1 <= phi, from the pi selections of a single pi);
 (c) Pi(A, phi) : U gives A : U and Cons phi.

Lemma 2.4 (typed elements are consistent).  u : a gives Cons u.
Proof.  Induction.  Bot, Univ: Cons om, Cons U and 1.3.  Pi: Cons A by
induction, Cons phi premise, 1.4, 1.3.  Lam: premise and 1.3.

Proposition 2.5 (= paper 2.17, upward monotonicity in the type).
u : a, a' <= a and a' : U give u : a'.  And: ent f A phi, A' <= A,
phi' <= phi and A' : U give ent f A' phi'.
Proof.  Simultaneous induction on the two forms.
 Bot: same rule with a'.
 Univ, Pi: a' <= a == U, so a' == U by 2.3(a).
 Lam: a' <= Pi(A, phi); by 2.3(b) a' == Pi(A1, phi1), A1 <= A,
   phi1 <= phi, A1 : U; the entries move to (A1, phi1) by induction;
   Pi(A1, phi1) : U from a' : U by 2.1.
 ent (x -> v): x : A gives x : A' by induction; phi' <= phi <= x -> y;
   v : y unchanged.  ent om, ent meet: immediate / induction.
(As in MIN, the hypothesis a' : U is needed: om : om, but om is not of
type lambda(f).)

Proposition 2.6 (= paper 2.16, joins).  u : a, v : a and
Cons (u /\ v) give u /\ v : a.
Proof.  Induction on u : a, generation (2.3) for v : a.
 - u or v by Bot: u /\ v == the other one; 2.1.
 - Univ/Univ: u /\ v == U.
 - Univ/Pi, Pi/Univ: u /\ v has a univ and a pi component, against (c1)
   of Cons (u /\ v) (1.2).
 - a == U against a == Pi (Univ or Pi against Lam): no confusion.
 - Pi/Pi: u == Pi(A, phi), v == Pi(B, psi).  Cons (u /\ v) gives, by 1.3
   and (c4) at the selection of both pi components, Cons (A /\ B) and
   Cons (phi /\ psi).  By induction A /\ B : U.  By 2.5 the entries of
   phi and psi are typed over A /\ B (A /\ B <= A, B), so
   ent (phi /\ psi) (A /\ B) (om -> U); u /\ v == Pi(A /\ B, phi /\ psi)
   (pi covariant, Pi(A,phi) /\ Pi(B,psi) <= Pi(A /\ B, phi /\ psi)).
 - Lam/Lam: u == f over (A, phi), v == g over (B, psi), Pi(A,phi) ==
   a == Pi(B, psi), so A == B, phi == psi (2.3); the entries of g move to
   (A, phi) by 2.5 (A : U by 2.3(c)); u /\ v == f /\ g, consistent by 1.3.

Lemma 2.7 (entries at a selection).  Let ent g A phi, Cons g, Cons phi,
and ASel g d t (a selection of entries of g), x : A, x <= d.  Then
t : y for some y with phi <= x -> y.
Proof.  Induction on the selection.  Empty: t = om, y = om (om : om).
One entry x' -> v: v : y' with phi <= x' -> y' <= x -> y' (x <= x').
Meet of two selections: by induction t1 : y1, t2 : y2; y = y1 /\ y2,
phi <= x -> y (le-arr-meet).  y : U by 2.6 at U (y1, y2 : U by 2.2;
Cons y: phi <= x -> y gives an arrow selection d'', t'' of phi with
x <= d'', t'' <= y; Cons x (2.4) gives Cons d'' (1.3), Cons t'' (c5),
Cons y (1.3)).  t1, t2 : y by 2.5; t1 /\ t2 : y by 2.6, Cons (t1 /\ t2)
by (c5) of g at the selection (Cons (d1 /\ d2) from Cons x, x <= d1 /\ d2).

Lemma 2.8 (the values of a typed function; what is used of Prop. 2.15).
f : Pi(A, phi), ASel f d t, x : A and x <= d give y with phi <= x -> y
and w <= t with w : y  (the part t of f(x) is below a typed element of
phi(x); typing is not downward closed in the element, so w = t is not
possible in general).
Proof.  Generation.  Bot: f om-like, so t is om-like (Types.asel-tr to
om), y = om, w = t.  Univ, Pi: no confusion.  Lam: f == g over (B, psi)
with Pi(B,psi) == Pi(A,phi); move the entries to (A, phi) (2.5); the
selection of f is transported to g (Types.asel-tr): d <=s d', t' <=s t;
Lemma 2.7 at d', t' (Cons phi by 2.3(c)), w = t'.
