Notation (Syntax.seg).  ren r M is the traversal of the renaming kit,
sub s M that of the substitution kit; liftR r, liftS s their lifts under
a binder; wk M = ren fs M; M[N] = sub (sub1 N) M with sub1 N 0 = N,
sub1 N (i+1) = var i.  Equalities of terms are Id (Tm n).  "By
definition" below means: the two sides compute to the same term.
The facts used by definition, for all i:
  liftR r 0 = 0,          liftR r (i+1) = (r i)+1,
  liftS s 0 = var 0,      liftS s (i+1) = wk (s i),
  wk (var i) = var (i+1), sub s (var i) = s i.

4.1 Equations on terms (SynLemmas.seg)

E0 (extensionality).  For any kit, s i = s' i for all i gives
trav s M = trav s' M.  Induction on M; under a binder lift s and lift s'
agree pointwise by cases on the variable.

E1 (a traversal after a renaming).  For any kit,
trav s (ren r M) = trav (s . r) M.
Induction on M.  Under a binder lift s (liftR r i) = lift (s . r) i by
definition in both cases (0 |-> vz; i+1 |-> wkv (s (r i))), then E0.

E2 (a renaming after a substitution).  ren r (sub s M) = sub (ren r . s) M.
Induction on M.  Under a binder, at 0 both sides are var 0; at i+1 the
claim is
  (WR)  ren (liftR r) (wk N) = wk (ren r N):
both sides are ren (fs . r) N by E1 (with the renaming kit), the left
since liftR r . fs = fs . r by definition.

E3 (a substitution after a substitution).
sub s2 (sub s1 M) = sub (sub s2 . s1) M.
Induction on M.  Under a binder, at 0 both sides are var 0; at i+1 the
claim is
  (WS)  sub (liftS s) (wk N) = wk (sub s N):
both sides are sub (wk . s) N, the left by E1 (substitution kit) since
liftS s . fs = wk . s by definition, the right by E2.

E4 (identity).  sub var M = M.  Induction on M; under a binder liftS var
agrees with var pointwise by definition, then E0.

Consequences.
D1  (wk e)[a] = e.   E1: sub (sub1 a . fs) e, and sub1 a . fs = var
    by definition; E4.
D2  sub s (B[a]) = (sub (liftS s) B)[sub s a].  By E3 both sides are
    sub t B: at 0 both t's give sub s a by definition; at i+1, s i
    and (wk (s i))[sub s a], equal by D1.
D3  ren r (B[a]) = (ren (liftR r) B)[ren r a].  E2 on the left, E1 on
    the right; the two substitutions agree pointwise by definition.
D4  (ren (liftR fs) e)[var 0] = e.  E1: the substitution
    sub1 (var 0) . liftR fs is var by definition; E4.
(WR) and (WS) are stated as lemmas wk-ren and wk-sub: they are the
commutations with weakening used by the typing lemmas below.

4.2 Renaming of derivations (Weaken.seg)

A renaming r : Fin n -> Fin m is typed from G to H, RenOk G H r, when
lookup H (r i) = ren r (lookup G i) for every i.
 - fs is typed from G to (G, C), by definition of lookup.
 - liftR r is typed from (G, A) to (H, ren r A) when r is typed: at 0,
   wk (ren r A) = ren (liftR r) (wk A) by WR; at i+1,
   wk (lookup H (r i)) = wk (ren r (lookup G i)) by the hypothesis, and
   = ren (liftR r) (wk (lookup G i)) by WR.

Lemma R.  RenOk G H r and wf H give, from G |- M : A,
H |- ren r M : ren r A, and from G |- M = N : A,
H |- ren r M = ren r N : ren r A.
Proof.  One induction on the derivation (motive Top at wf G).  Each rule
is mapped to itself; under a binder the hypothesis wf (H, ren r A) is
wf-ext of the induction hypothesis at A, and liftR r is typed.  The
conclusion is transported along an equation of terms in: var (the
hypothesis RenOk), app, beta, the two app congruences (D3: renaming
commutes with M[a]), funext (wk-ren for f and g; liftR r 0 = 0).

Weakening: G |- C : U gives, by R with fs, from G |- J the judgement
(G, C) |- wk J (wf (G, C) by wf-ext).

4.3 Substitution of derivations (Subst.seg; P0 of 4.4 and context
    conversion are there too)

A substitution s : Fin g -> Tm h is typed from G to H, SubOk H G s, when
H |- s i : sub s (lookup G i) for every i.
 - Lift: SubOk H G s and H |- sub s A : U give
   SubOk (H, sub s A) (G, A) (liftS s): at 0, var 0 : wk (sub s A)
   by the variable rule, and wk (sub s A) = sub (liftS s) (wk A) by WS;
   at i+1, wk (s i) : wk (sub s (lookup G i)) by weakening, and WS.
   (The typing of sub s A is a hypothesis, given by the induction
   hypothesis at A: so there is no mutual induction.)
 - sub1: G |- A : U and G |- a : A give SubOk G (G, A) (sub1 a): at 0,
   a : A = (wk A)[a] by D1; at i+1, var i : lookup G i = (wk (lookup G i))[a]
   by D1 (needs wf G, 4.4).
 - Identity: wf G gives SubOk G G var, by E4.

Lemma S.  SubOk H G s and wf H give, from G |- M : A,
H |- sub s M : sub s A, and the same for conversions.
Proof.  As Lemma R, with the lift above; the variable rule is the
hypothesis itself (sub s (var i) = s i by definition); the transports in
app, beta, the app congruences are D2, in funext WS.

4.4 Presupposition and context conversion (Presup.seg)

P0  Every derivation of G |- J gives wf G.  Induction: the rules with a
    premise wf G return it, the others take it from their first premise.
P1  wf (G, A) gives G |- A : U (inversion of wf-ext: a motive by cases
    on n, Ctx (n+1) being a pair).  So wf G gives G |- lookup G i : U
    for every i, by induction on n: at 0, weakening of A; at i+1,
    weakening of the induction hypothesis at G.
P2  G |- M : A gives G |- A : U.  Induction: var P1, conv its premise,
    U by U, Pi by U (wf from P0), lam by Pi, app by S with sub1.
Context conversion.  G |- A : U, G |- A' : U, G |- A = A' : U give
SubOk (G, A') (G, A) var: at 0, var 0 : wk A' and wk A' = wk A by
weakening (symmetry), so var 0 : wk A by conversion, wk A : U by
weakening; at i+1 by the variable rule; then E4 for sub var (wk A).
By Lemma S and E4, (G, A) |- J gives (G, A') |- J.
P3  G |- M = N : A gives G |- M : A and G |- N : A.  Induction.  refl,
    sym, trans, conv: directly.  beta: app of lam on the left, S with
    sub1 on the right.  Pi: Pi A B by the rule; for Pi A' B', A' : U by
    the induction hypothesis at A = A', B' over (G, A') by context
    conversion.  funext: its premises.  app-fun: app on both sides.
    app-arg: on the right a' : A, and B[a'] = B[a] : U, obtained from
    app-arg at the function lam A B : Pi A U (B : U over (G, A)) and
    beta at a and a' (MIN's trick, as there is no congruence for
    substitution); then conversion.

4.5 Two related substitutions (Cross.seg)

s and s' are equal from G to H, SubEq H G s s', when
H |- s i = s' i : sub s (lookup G i) for every i.

Lemma X.  SubOk H G s, SubOk H G s', SubEq H G s s', wf H and
G |- M : A give H |- sub s M = sub s' M : sub s A.
Proof.  Induction on the typing derivation (motive Top at wf and
conversion judgements), the motive quantified over H, s, s'.
 - var: the hypothesis SubEq.  U: refl.
 - conv: conversion along S at the premise A = B.
 - Under a binder (Pi, lam, app): the lifts are typed from (G, A) to
   (H, sub s A); liftS s' only to (H, sub s' A), so it is moved to
   (H, sub s A) by context conversion along the induction hypothesis
   sub s A = sub s' A : U.  The lifts are equal: at 0 refl of var 0, at
   i+1 weakening of SubEq; with WS for the types.
 - Pi: the Pi-congruence rule; its premises are the induction
   hypotheses and their presuppositions (P3).
 - lam: there is no congruence for lam, so by funext: with
   L = lam (sub s A) (sub (liftS s) M), L' likewise with s',
     wk L applied to var 0  =  sub (liftS s) M      (beta, D4)
                            =  sub (liftS s') M     (induction)
                            =  wk L' applied to var 0 (beta, D4, context
                                                     conversion, and the
                                                     codomains equal by
                                                     induction at B).
   The typing of L' at Pi (sub s A) (sub (liftS s) B) is the lam rule
   at s' and conversion along the Pi-congruence.
 - app: app-fun (induction at f) then app-arg (induction at a), the type
   of sub s' f moved to Pi (sub s A) (sub (liftS s) B) by conversion as
   in lam; D2 for the type.

4.6 Head reduction (Reduction.seg)

One head step is a FUNCTION hstep : Tm n -> Opt (Tm n):
  hstep (app (lam A M) N) = some M[N]
  hstep (app M N)         = app (-) N of hstep M   (M not a lam)
  hstep M                 = none                    (var, U, Pi, lam).
M ~> M' is hstep M = some M'; M ~>* M' its reflexive-transitive closure
(an inductive family).  Determinism is then by construction:
 - M ~> M1 and M ~> M2 give M1 = M2 (some is injective);
 - U, Pi A B, lam A M do not reduce (none is not some);
 - so T ~>* N1 and T ~>* N2 with N1, N2 among U, Pi A B give N1 = N2
   (induction on the first closure), and Pi A B ~>* N gives N = Pi A B.
 - M ~>* M' gives app M N ~>* app M' N (hstep at app of a non-lam;
   a reducing M is not a lam).
The relation uses this untyped reduction; the typed conversions are in
the relation only where they are read off (4.8).

4.7 Domain and codomain of a filter (LogRel.seg, first part)

For a filter X:
  IsPi X     pi om om in X;
  Dom X    = { a | om <= a, or pi a om in X }   (OmOr, Filter.seg)
  Cod X    = { b | om <= b, or pi om b in X }
  Fam X W  = app (Cod X) W                     for a filter W.
Pi is covariant, so pi a b in X iff pi a om and pi om b in X: X's Pi part
is Dom X x Cod X.  All three respect == of filters (and W).

Lemma 4.7.1 (the semantic Pi).  For A a filter, G = fam B rho:
 (a) Dom (pi A G) == A, and IsPi (pi A G);
 (b) if W : A (HasTy), then Fam (pi A G) W == [[B]] (rho, W).
Proof.  (a) pi a om in pi A G iff some pi a' b' <= pi a om with a' in A
(the om summand is excluded: om is not below pi), iff a in A.  pi om om
from a = om, b = om.
(b) c=: t in Fam: g <= w -> t with pi om g in pi A G, w in W; g is above
the family part b' of an element of pi A G, whose entries x -> v have
v in G x; by Lemma 3.1 (selection) at w, t in G w = [[B]] (rho, prin w),
included in [[B]] (rho, W) (3.4, prin w c= W).  =c: t in [[B]] (rho, W);
by continuity in the last variable (cont-last) t in [[B]] (rho, prin w)
for some w in W; W : A gives a typed w' <= w in W, w' : a, a in A, and
t in [[B]] (rho, prin w') (3.4); then pi a (w' -> t) in pi A G, so
w' -> t in Cod, and t in app (Cod) W.

4.8 The relation (LogRel.seg)

Fix the context D of n variables (the relation is not Kripke: all terms
live in D).  For a fuel N, a type filter X, an element filter Y, terms T
(type), M, M', and type terms T, T':

  TyR 0 Y T T'           = 1          TmR 0 X Y T M M'     = 1
  TyR (N+1) Y T T'       = TyR N Y T T'
                         * (univ in Y  ->  T ~>* U and T' ~>* U)
                         * (IsPi Y    ->  PiTy N Y T T')
  TmR (N+1) X Y T M M'   = TmR N X Y T M M'
                         * (univ in X  ->  TyR (N+1) Y M M')
                         * (IsPi X    ->  PiTm N X Y T M M')

PiTy N Y T T': there are A0, B0, A0', B0' with
  T ~>* Pi A0 B0,  T' ~>* Pi A0' B0',  D |- A0 = A0' : U,
  D, A0 |- B0 = B0' : U,  TyR N (Dom Y) A0 A0',  and for every filter W
  and terms P, P' with W : Dom Y, D |- P = P' : A0,
  TmR N (Dom Y) W A0 P P', the three
     TyR N (Fam Y W) B0[P] B0[P'],  TyR N (Fam Y W) B0'[P] B0'[P'],
     TyR N (Fam Y W) B0[P] B0'[P'].
PiTm N X Y T M M': for all A0, B0 with T ~>* Pi A0 B0, and W, P, P' as
above (at Dom X, A0), the three, all at the type B0[P],
     TmR N (Fam X W) (app Y W) B0[P] (M P)  (M P'),
     TmR N (Fam X W) (app Y W) B0[P] (M' P) (M' P'),
     TmR N (Fam X W) (app Y W) B0[P] (M P)  (M' P').

Recursion is on N only (B2): the witnesses Dom Y, Fam Y W are not
smaller than Y.  TyR (N+1) is defined from TyR N and TmR N, then
TmR (N+1) from TmR N and TyR (N+1).  The conjunct "R N" in R (N+1) makes
R (N+1) => R N a projection (B3: Kripke over the fuel).

Why filters (departing from B4 of the plan).  With compact witnesses a, u
and clauses at all upper bounds (Le a univ, Le a (pi A phi)), monotonicity
toward less information is free, but adequacy also needs it toward MORE
information: in the cases Pi and lam the argument comes related at the
domain A of an upper bound of the type witness, while the related
substitution for the new variable asks for every type witness in [[A]] rho
(the paper's MonoTypeIncr, and with it Join).  The paper and MIN prove
these laws (MIN Validity/Mono, Stability, Levels: about 3k lines), and the
Lam case of MonoTypeIncr needs the typing of the arguments at both
domains.  With the semantic filters themselves as witnesses, adequacy
relates exactly [[M]] rho at exactly [[A]] rho: every law below keeps the
witnesses fixed (up to ==), no law needs a typing premise, and there is
no monotonicity, join or stability.  The relation at infinite elements
(Coquand-Huber) is the same definition.

4.9 Laws (LogRel.seg; each by induction on N, the case N+1 using the laws
    at N; the order of the list is the order of proof at N+1)

L0 (==).  X == X', Y == Y' give TyR N Y T T' <=> TyR N Y' T T' and
   TmR N X Y T M M' <=> TmR N X' Y' T M M'.  (Dom, Fam, app respect ==;
   the argument hypotheses are compared by L0 at N in both directions.)
L1 (trivial element).  If every element of Y is om-like, TyR N Y T T' and
   TmR N X Y T M M' hold.  (univ in Y, IsPi Y are false; app Y W is
   trivial, then L1 at N and L0.)
L2 (diagonal).  TyR N Y T T' gives TyR N Y T T; TmR N X Y T M M' gives
   TmR N X Y T M M.  (The first of the three conclusions; refl of A0, B0,
   typed by presupposition 4.4.)
L3 (symmetry of TyR).  TyR N Y T T' gives TyR N Y T' T.
   Pi: reductions swapped; conversions by symmetry, B0' = B0 moved to
   the context (D, A0') by context conversion; domain by L3 at N; for
   arguments at A0': moved to A0 by L5 at N (with L3 at N for the
   domain), then the second, first conclusions and, for B0'[P] = B0[P'],
   the third at the arguments (P', P) (L4 at N) and L3 at N.
L4 (symmetry of TmR).  TmR N X Y T M M' gives TmR N X Y T M' M.
   U: L3 at N+1.  Pi: second, first, and M' P = M P' by L6 at N from
   M' P = M P (L4 at N of the third at (P, P), L2) and M P = M P'.
L5 (conversion).  TyR N X T T2 and TmR N X Y T M M' give
   TmR N X Y T2 M M'.  Pi: T2 ~>* Pi A2 B2 is the reduct recorded in
   TyR (determinism); arguments at A2 are moved to A0 (L5 at N, L3 at N
   at the domain; conversion of P = P' along A2 = A0); the conclusions
   at B0[P] are moved to B2[P] by L5 at N along the third conclusion of
   the family at (P, P) (L2).
L6 (transitivity).  TyR N Y T1 T2, TyR N Y T2 T3 give TyR N Y T1 T3, and
   the same for TmR at a fixed type T.  Reducts of T2: determinism.
   Conversions by transitivity (context conversion for B).  Family:
   B1[P] = B3[P'] from B1[P] = B2[P'] and B2[P'] = B3[P'] (the second's
   third conclusion at (P', P'), moved from A1 to A2 by L5); terms:
   M1 P = M3 P' from M1 P = M2 P (at (P, P)) and M2 P = M3 P'.
L7 (head expansion).  M ~>* M1, M' ~>* M1' and TmR N X Y T M1 M1' give
   TmR N X Y T M M'; the same for TyR.  (Reductions compose; app M P ~>*
   app M1 P.)  Expansion on one side only is the case M' = M1'.
L8 (to type).  univ in X and TmR (N+1) X Y T M M' give TyR (N+1) Y M M'.

4.10 Adequacy (paper Thm 2.35; Adequacy.seg)

Related substitutions.  s, s' : D -> G (Fin m -> Tm n) are related over
an environment rho fitting G at fuel N when SubOk s, SubOk s', SubEq s s'
(4.3, 4.5) and for every variable i, with A_i = lookup G i,
    TmR N ([[A_i]] rho) (rho i) (A_i[s]) (s i) (s' i)   and
    TyR N ([[A_i]] rho) (A_i[s]) (A_i[s']).
Then (s, s) is related (L2 at each variable; SubEq refl), and so is
(s', s): L4 then L5 along TyR A_i[s'] A_i[s] (L3); SubEq by symmetry and
conversion along A_i[s] = A_i[s'] (Lemma X at lookup G i : U, 4.4).
Without the TyR part the case sym below would need adequacy at the types
A_i, which are not premises of the derivation.

Theorem.  rho fits G, s, s' related over rho at fuel N:
    G |- M = M' : A  gives  TmR N ([[A]] rho) ([[M]] rho) A[s] M[s] M'[s']
                     and    TyR N ([[A]] rho) A[s] A[s'],
and G |- M : A the same with M' = M.  At A = U, L8 turns the first into
TyR N ([[M]] rho) M[s] M'[s'] (for N = 0 both are 1).
Proof.  Induction on the derivation; in each case induction on N (the
conjunct R N by the same case at N; N = 0 is 1).  The semantics are
moved along == by L0 and the equations of Sem/Sound (sem-*-eq,
subst1-sem, Beta, 4.7.1).  The syntactic conversions in the Pi clauses
are Lemma X (4.5) and S (4.3).  The second part (TyR of the type) is
built from the premises typing the type: var (related substitutions),
conv (B : U), Pi (U), lam (as the case Pi at A, B), app and beta (B at
(s, a[s]), (s', a[s'])), funext (f : Pi A B), the others from their
premise at the same type.
Extension.  If s, s' are related over rho, W : [[A]] rho, D |- P = P' :
A[s], TmR N [[A]] W A[s] P P' and TyR N [[A]] A[s] A[s'], then (s, P),
(s', P') are related over (rho, W) (which fits (G, A)): SubOk of (s', P')
by conversion along A[s] = A[s'] (Lemma X); the old variables by
wk-sem (L0) and (wk A_i)[(s, P)] = A_i[s] (D1).
 - var: the hypothesis on s, s'.
 - conv (M : A, A = B): [[A]] = [[B]] (soundness); TyR N [[A]] A[s] B[s]
   by induction at (s, s); L5.
 - U: univ in univF; TyR (N+1) univF U U: U ~>* U, univF is not a Pi.
 - Pi, eq-Pi: TyR (N+1) [[Pi A B]] (Pi A B)[s] (Pi A' B')[s']: univ is not
   in [[Pi A B]] (pi-univ-clash); IsPi, Dom = [[A]] rho (4.7.1);
   reductions of length 0; conversions by Lemma X (Pi) or S and X
   (eq-Pi); domain by induction at N; family: for W : [[A]], P = P' at
   A[s], TmR N [[A]] W A[s] P P', the substitutions (s, P), (s', P') are
   related over (rho, W) at N (W : [[A]] makes (rho, W) fit (G, A));
   induction at B gives the third conclusion; the first from (s, P),
   (s, P') ((s, s) related); the second from the first and third by L4,
   L6 (all at Fam = [[B]] (rho, W), 4.7.1(b)).
 - lam: TmR (N+1) [[Pi A B]] [[lam A M]] (Pi A B)[s] ..: univ not in the
   type; T = Pi A[s] B[liftS s] is normal (4.6), so A0, B0 are these;
   app [[lam A M]] W == [[M]] (rho, W) (Beta, W : [[A]]); the body by
   induction at fuel N with (s, P), (s', P'), and head expansion L7
   ((lam ..) P ~> M[liftS s][P] = M[(s, P)]); the third conclusion
   directly, the first at (s, P), (s, P'), the second by L4, L6 from the
   third at (P, P) and at (P, P').
 - app: the function at fuel N+2 (its Pi clause gives the application at
   N+1), the argument at N+1; Fam [[Pi A B]] [[N]] == [[B]] (rho, [[N]]) ==
   [[B[N]]] rho; B[liftS s][N[s]] = B[N][s] (D2).
 - beta: L7 on the left, then the body as in lam, with (s, N[s]),
   (s', N[s']).
 - funext: the Pi clause from the premise (G, A |- f x = g x : B) at
   (s, P), (s', P') (third); the first and second from the typings of f,
   g (their own Pi clauses at N+1).
 - refl: the typing.  sym: the premise at (s', s), L4, then L5 along
   TyR A[s'] A[s] (L3 of the second part at (s, s')).  trans: the first
   premise at (s, s), the second at (s, s'), L6.  eq-conv: as conv.
 - app-fun: the function equation at N+2 and (s, s'), its third
   conclusion at the argument a[s], a[s'].  app-arg: the function typing
   at N+2, the third conclusion at a[s], a'[s'] (S, then X, for
   D |- a[s] = a'[s'] : A[s]).

4.11 Consequences (Consequences.seg)

The bottom environment rho0 (prin om at every variable) fits every
well-formed context (induction on wf G, with soundness: [[A]] rho0 : U
gives typed elements, and om is below each); var, var are related over
rho0 at every fuel by L1 (prin om is trivial), with SubOk, SubEq of the

Corollary 2.37 (Pi injectivity).  G |- Pi A B = Pi A' B' : U gives
G |- A = A' : U and G, A |- B = B' : U.
Proof.  Adequacy at N = 2 with rho0 and the identity, at type U: L8 gives
TyR 2 [[Pi A B]] rho0 (Pi A B)[id] (Pi A' B')[id]; IsPi (4.7.1); Pi A B
is normal, so its Pi clause records the two conversions, at A[id] = A
and B[liftS id] = B (E4).
Theorem 2.38 (subject reduction).  G |- M : A and M ~> M' give
G |- M' : A: generation for app up to conversion, Pi injectivity, and the
substitution lemma (4.3).

The Pi clause of TmR (N+1) gives the applications at fuel N.  So in the
case app, the conclusion at N needs the function at N+1, hence the
substitution related at N+1: adequacy "related at N => related at N"
fails.  It cannot be repaired by asking more fuel of the substitution
(N + height of the derivation): in the case lam the argument P comes
related at N only, and the body may apply it.  Example: lam x. x y, with
x : Pi A A; the clause at N+1 asks P y related at N for P related at N,
and P's own clause gives P y at N-1 only.  The same holds with the
compact witnesses of the plan: B3 (the conjunct R N in R (N+1)) gives
R (N+1) => R N, while the application needs R N => R (N+1) at the
witnesses at hand.  In the paper this is stability (Cor. 2.33): the
witnesses at stage n are in C_n (depth <= n, also the arguments v in the
Pi clause), the result of applying a stage-(n+1) witness is in C_n, and
the relation at stage n+1 restricted to C_n is the relation at stage n.
With filters (possibly infinite elements) there is no such bound, and
lam x. x y is genuinely not related at a fixed fuel.

definition (LogRel.seg, Laws.seg), which keep the witnesses fixed and so
need no stability.  What changes: the quantifications must be bounded by
the stage (compact witnesses of depth <= N, as C_N) and a stability lemma
proved, or another way found to make the fuel of the arguments match the

4.14 The relation at compact witnesses (4.13 worked out; replaces 4.8,
     4.9 and 4.10-4.12; 4.6 and the filter facts of 4.7 stay)

The quantifications of the Pi clauses are over the ARROW SELECTIONS OF
THE WITNESS ITSELF (MIN: Selection g u v, entries of the code), not over
all arguments of the domain (paper).  The arguments and values are then
parts of the witness, of smaller depth (Types.asel-md, psel-md), so the
recursion on the fuel stops at the depth of the witness and the relation
is independent of the fuel above it (Lemma F, the stability of the paper,
proved as Consistency's Lemma 1.1).  The application in adequacy then
needs no fuel bookkeeping (4.12 disappears: there is no fuel left).
Since the arguments are read off the element, the relation has NO TYPE
WITNESS: the type is needed only by the laws comparing two types (L3-L6),
where it is a typing hypothesis u : c (Typing.Tp).  So the paper's
MonoTypeIncr / MonoTypeDecr and Join have no counterpart.

Notation.  A selection (d, t) of u is ASel u d t: d, t the meets of the
domains and codomains of chosen arrow components of u (u <= d -> t,
asel-le); (A, phi) a pi selection of c (PSel c A phi) when c has a pi
component (Sel c (pi f g)); then c <= pi A phi (psel-le).

4.14.1 Definition (context D fixed; fuel N; witnesses c, u : Ty)

  TyR 0 c T T' = 1,    TmR 0 u T M M' = 1,
  TyR (N+1) c T T' = (Sel c univ  ->  T ~>* U  and  T' ~>* U)
                   * (for every pi component of c and pi selection
                      (A, phi) of c:  PiTy N A phi T T')
  PiTy N A phi T T': there are A0, B0, A1, B1 with
      T ~>* Pi A0 B0,  T' ~>* Pi A1 B1,  D |- A0 = A1 : U,
      D, A0 |- B0 = B1 : U,  TyR N A A0 A1,  and for every selection
      (d, y) of phi with Cons d, and P, P' with D |- P = P' : A0 and
      TmR N d A0 P P', the three
         TyR N y B0[P] B0[P'],  TyR N y B1[P] B1[P'],  TyR N y B0[P] B1[P'].
  TmR (N+1) u T M M' = TyR (N+1) u M M'
                   * (for all A0, B0 with T ~>* Pi A0 B0, every selection
                      (d, t) of u with Cons d, and P, P' with
                      D |- P = P' : A0 and TmR N d A0 P P', the three
                         TmR N t B0[P] (M P) (M P'),  TmR N t B0[P] (M' P) (M' P'),
                         TmR N t B0[P] (M P) (M' P')).

TyR (N+1) uses TmR N; TmR (N+1) uses TyR (N+1) at the same witness and
TmR N.  The first component of TmR is the paper's clause at U (an
element of U is a type); the second its clause at Pi.  For a function
witness the first is vacuous (no univ or pi component), for a type
witness the second (its arrow selections are om-like, L1).

Lemma F (enough fuel).  md u < a and md u < b give R a <=> R b at the
witness u (R = TyR or TmR, all terms).
Proof.  Induction on a, b.  At a+1, b+1 the clauses compare the relations
at a and b at parts of u (d, t, A, y), of depth <= md u - 1 < a, b: by
induction in both directions (the arguments occur negatively).  When
md u = 0 the selections are om-only, and both sides hold by L1.
Then  TyR c = TyR (md c + 1) c,  TmR u = TmR (md u + 1) u,  and by F
each unfolds to the clauses above with TyR, TmR at the parts (the
fixpoint, as Consistency's Lemma 1.2); the definition is then locked.
