# Chapter 2 in Unit

The numbering follows the local [book](book.pdf). The Unit development uses univalence and replacement. Function extensionality, propositional truncation, set quotients and higher truncations are derived in that segment. The formalisation uses Unit’s ordinary inductive types for naturals, booleans, lists and finite sums. The book’s excluded-middle and resizing principles occur as propositions when their relationship is studied in §2.18.

## Computation on generators

The book specifies judgemental computation for the truncations in Definitions 2.16.1 and 2.22.4 and the HIT presentation in Definition 2.22.16. It also uses this strength in Theorem 2.22.9 and the construction following Theorem 2.22.13. In Unit the replacement hypothesis supplies an equivalence by precomposition. Its inverse supplies extension and its section law supplies a path on each generator. For example, `Truncation.Trunc.beta` has the dependent type expressing `extend b (inc a) = b a`. A direct checker probe with symbolic inputs rejected `refl (b a)` for propositional truncation, set truncation and weakly constant descent. The corresponding path proofs check. The comparison of the two presentations of a quotient in Exercise 2.22.17 uses their elimination properties and needs no additional HIT. The identification of the component over a generator in Exercise 2.22.5 is likewise given by an explicit equivalence. ## Universe checks

For Exercise 2.25.8, Unit accepts the classification of families `A → U u` over `A : U v` under `u+ ≤ v`. The type of large maps with specified small fibres is in `U v+`. The test instantiates the target at the join `u+ ∨ v` and separately requires rejection at unconstrained levels `u, v`. Exercise 2.19.5 only needs `u ≤ v` to construct the small component of a locally small `X : U v`. The dependent truncation eliminator accepts unrelated source and motive levels.
