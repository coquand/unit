Selected-prime primitive element: small coefficient-field branch
================================================================

Status: mathematical simplification, NOT a checked Unit theorem.
Source: Lombardi--Quitte, Commutative Algebra: Constructive Methods,
English LQ.pdf, VI--1.9, pp. 307--308. The earlier proof plan is in
unit/doc/etale-field-reduction.tex.

Suppose C=k[x,z] is a finite-dimensional reduced algebra with a specified
prime p whose membership is decidable. The large-field branch still needs
the separable annihilators and the nonzero discriminant polynomial of
VI--1.9. The following simplifies only its small-field branch.

1. Bounded closure of the finitely many polynomial coefficients either
produces enough distinct base-field elements for the discriminant search,
or an explicitly finite coefficient subfield k0. This does not decide
whether k itself is finite.

2. In the latter case, B0=k0[x,z] is a finite set: monic annihilating
polynomials for x,z give a finite spanning list, and k0 is a finite set.
Equality is decidable because B0 is a subset of the finite-dimensional
k-algebra C. Enumerate its distinct values. B0 is reduced because C is.

3. The restricted prime p0=p intersect B0 is therefore finitely generated,
with explicit finite generators obtained by filtering that enumeration.
A finite reduced ring is von Neumann regular: for an element a, a repeated
power yields a^i(1-a^d)=0, and reducedness gives a(1-a^d)=0, hence a=a^2b
with b=a^(d-1). Equivalently use a monic equation over k0 and its first
nonzero coefficient. The witness must be constructed INSIDE B0; regularity
of C alone does not provide a witness in the subring.

4. Apply the already checked IdempotentIdeal lemma to p0, using regularity
of its finite generators. It gives e with e^2=e, e*p0=0 and 1-e in p0.
Properness implies e not in p0. The map B0 -> eB0 has kernel exactly p0:
primality gives the converse to annihilation. Thus F=eB0 is an explicitly
finite integral domain and hence a finite field. This chooses ONE factor
at the given prime; no global factor decomposition is needed.

5. A generator gamma of F's cyclic multiplicative group generates F over
k0. Since ex and ez lie in F, C_e=k[gamma]. For a separable annihilator,
P(T)=T^Q-T with Q=|F| suffices: every element of F satisfies it, and the
image of Q in F is zero, so P'(gamma)=-1. P may be much larger than the
minimal polynomial, which the local theorem does not need.

Remaining obligations: bounded-closure termination, enumeration and
reducedness/regularity of B0, actual construction of the selected factor,
finite-field multiplicative cyclicity, and the Frobenius polynomial law.
These are not supplied merely by the informal argument. The finite-field
cyclicity proof can be elementary: combine element orders to obtain an
element with maximal order, then use the polynomial root bound to show
all nonzero elements are powers of it. This still needs checked finite
arithmetic and order lemmas.

Benefit: in the small-field branch, one finite selected-factor construction
can replace the dynamic simulation of the field proof and repeated rank
splitting. The large-field trace/separability work is not removed.
