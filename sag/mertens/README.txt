Dedekind--Mertens: the direct proof in mertens.pdf
================================================

Source: A direct proof of the Dedekind-Mertens Lemma, April 5, 2006,
supplied as ../../../mertens.pdf.

Check from unit/:

    ./segc sag/mertens all

The checked theorem
-------------------

For every commutative ring A, every polynomial coefficient list p, and
Q = cons g q with m = length q, the development constructs explicit maps
in both directions between certificates of membership in

    F^(m+1) G       and       F^m H,

where F,G,H are the additive subgroups generated by the coefficients of
P,Q,PQ. It also constructs both inclusions for the ideals

    c(P)^(m+1)c(Q) = c(P)^m c(PQ).

Here m is a formal degree bound. Leading coefficients may vanish, and the
zero polynomial is included by taking g=0 and q=nil. P may be the empty
list. No degree computation, decidable equality, or nonzero assumption is
used. The two polynomial arguments are independent.

The ideal products are represented directly by their coefficient-product
generators and arbitrary ring multiples; this development does not use
the quotient-based ideal API elsewhere in the library. Equality of the
generated subgroups/ideals means mutual inclusion of membership
certificates, not identity of the certificate types.

Interface
---------

    module D = DedekindMertens[l].Over[A]
    D.theorem p q g word j

The output is a finite signed sum of generators of F^m H together with
an equality showing that its value is

    (f_i0 ... f_im) * g_j.

A word of length n is an n-tuple of natural-number coefficient indices.
The product of a word of length zero is 1. Coefficients beyond the end
of a list are zero. The certificates only use addition and negation;
there are no arbitrary ring scalars in this stronger subgroup theorem.

    module E = ContentFormula[l].Over[A,p,g,q]
    E.subgroup-forward a certificate
    E.subgroup-backward a certificate
    E.ideal-forward a certificate
    E.ideal-backward a certificate

ZeroProduct additionally proves that PQ=0 coefficientwise implies

    (f_i0 ... f_im) * g_j = 0

for every word and j. This is the coefficient-annihilation consequence
needed in the content argument for regularity.

Correspondence with the paper
-----------------------------

The paper removes the highest term of Q and uses descending induction
on i. The formalisation uses lowest-first coefficient lists, removes the
constant term, writes Q=g+X Q', and uses ascending induction on i.
These are the same two inductions with the coefficient order reversed.

For m>0 put K=F^m H. The inner statement is

    f_i F^m G' <= K.

From the statements at indices below i, the coefficient identity for
PQ gives F^m f_i g <= K (head-product). The outer induction hypothesis
F^m G' <= F^(m-1)c_add(PQ'), multiplied by f_i, then proves the inner
statement at i. The outer step finishes by treating g and the
coefficients of Q' separately. Thus the constant coefficient case,
corresponding to the last coefficient case implicit in the paper,
is included explicitly.

Files
-----

AdditiveSpan: explicit signed sums, their universal mapping property,
closure and cancellation in an additive subgroup, and bounded induction.
CoefficientProducts: product-coefficient sums with their index bounds,
words of coefficient indices and their products.
DedekindMertens: outer induction, inner induction, and the strong inclusion.
IdealSpan: arbitrary ring multiples of a generating family.
ContentFormula: both subgroup inclusions and both ideal inclusions.
ZeroProduct: coefficient annihilation when PQ=0.

Dependencies are relative symlinks to the existing polynomial library
and to jacobian/FiniteJets.seg. Those files are unchanged.

Scope and foundations
---------------------

The proof uses Unit's primitive data eliminators and equality rules.
It adds no axiom, univalence/replacement parameter, choice, excluded
middle, or noetherian hypothesis. The full check on October 25, 2026
reported 14 modules, 0 failed (including the imported modules).

This directory proves the univariate theorem in mertens.pdf. The
multivariate content lemma and the localization argument of CAR1.pdf
are not yet connected to it. The separate Jacobian development still
has its explicitly stated annihilator-stabilization hypothesis.

An explanatory note, ending with the exact formalisation status, is in
../../doc/dedekind-mertens.tex (and the compiled PDF alongside it).
