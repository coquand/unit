2. **The field case**: (E/mE)[1/s] = A[1/s-bar] is a product of factors

## Sources

- `~/CANONICITY/decisif.pdf`: H. Lombardi, *Un théorème décisif*
  (arXiv 2506.07098).
- `~/COLL/LQ.pdf`: Lombardi–Quitté, *Commutative algebra: constructive
  methods*, Chapter VI §1 (Étale algebras over a discrete field) and §5
  (strictly étale algebras).
- Cited in decisif, not available locally: Quitté–Lombardi 2025,
  *Identités algébriques permettant de démontrer qu'une algèbre libre finie
  nette est traciquement étale* (arXiv 2506.03851).

Vocabulary (both sources): over a discrete field K, *étale* (LQ VI 1.1,
decisif 1.1) means strictly finite (a finite K-basis) with invertible
discriminant ("traciquement étale"); *nette* means unramified,
Omega_{A/K} = 0, i.e. the transposed Jacobian is surjective (decisif 3.1).
Our `FormallyEtale.Etale` is formally étale (unique lifting along
square-zero ideals) plus a presentation.

## The three layers

Let C = (E/mE)[1/s] (the special fibre, k = R/m discrete).

1. **C strictly finite, with decidable equality.**  Over a discrete field
   linear algebra is explicit once a basis is known; a merely finite
   K-algebra need not have one (LQ VI §1, first paragraph).  A = B/mB is only
   finitely generated over k (the b_i), so this does not come from ZMT.
2. **C separable** (discriminant invertible).
3. **C a product of monogenic separable factors** (or: one monogenic factor
   containing the prime q-bar, which is all `TheoremLocal` uses).

### Layers 1-2: decisif Theorem 4.4

*Over a discrete field every net (unramified) f.p. algebra is strictly
finite and étale.*  E étale over R gives E/mE net over k (decisif Lemma 3.4,
base change), so the field-case hypothesis could become a theorem.
- Strict finiteness = Noether dimension <= 0 (decisif Thm 4.5, via
  [ACMC IV-8.16]).  Proof by contradiction: if r > 0, pass to
  L = K(Y_1..Y_r), infinite, so C' = S^-1 A = L[x] is monogenic (primitive
  element), and -(d_1 f)/f'(x) extends d/dY_1 to a nonzero derivation of C',
  contradicting Omega = 0.  This is the **indeterminate trick, used
  legitimately**: the statement is decidable and proved by refuting its
  negation, so nothing has to come back from K(Y) to K.
- Separability: decisif Lemma 4.2 (a net free algebra of finite rank is
  étale), from the 2025 Quitté–Lombardi preprint (explicit identities).
- Cost: Noether position/dimension for polynomial systems (heavy), the
  derivation-extension argument, Lemma 4.2 (unknown until the preprint is
  read), and the link Omega = 0 <-> our formal unramifiedness (standard,
  moderate).

### Layer 3: LQ Theorem VI 1.9 (primitive element)

1. If K is infinite or B is a discrete field, B = K[b] = K[T]/(f), f
   separable.  2. In general B is a finite product of monogenic étale
   algebras.

Proof of 1 (the indeterminate trick again): with x, z having separable
minimal polynomials f, g and C = K[X,Z]/(f, g), adjoin indeterminates a, b;
the characteristic polynomial h_{a,b} of a xi + b zeta over K[a,b] has
discriminant
  d(a,b) = prod_{(i,j) /= (k,l)} (a (x_i - x_k) + b (z_j - z_l)),
leading monomial (a^{n^2-n} disc f)^p (b^{p^2-p} disc g)^n
(computed in a double universal splitting algebra), so d has an
invertible coefficient.  Choose alpha, beta in K with d(alpha, beta) /= 0;
then the characteristic polynomial of alpha xi + beta zeta is separable and
K[alpha xi + beta zeta] = C (LQ VI Lemma 1.2).

**Where the trick stops.**  The theorem produces *data* over K (a generator,
eps_i, P_i).  Over K(a,b) the generator is a xi + b zeta; coming back needs
a value (alpha, beta) in K avoiding the zeros of d, i.e. K with more than
about [B : K]^2 elements.  Passing to R(T) for the whole Theorem local has
the same problem: standard étale over R(T) does not descend to R without
specialising T in k.  For small K the book uses other arguments:
- B a field: enumerate until a good (alpha, beta) appears or the
  characteristic is a prime p; then the coefficients generate a finite
  field K_0, and K_0[x, z] is generated by a generator of its (cyclic)
  multiplicative group;
- general B (item 2): when the field argument "stumbles", B is not a field
  and an idempotent e /= 0, 1 appears; split and induct on [B : K].

Small residue fields are not a corner case: R = Z_(2) has k = F_2, and the
note's examples over Z have small residue fields.  Restricting to the factor
containing q-bar does not avoid this: that factor may be a field such as
F_8 over F_2, which needs a primitive element.

Cost of LQ 1.9:
- the generic step needs universal splitting algebras and the
  discriminant formula (LQ III, Prop. III-5.10, Fact III-5.11); `unit` has
  determinants, adjugates, characteristic polynomials and Cayley–Hamilton
  (`sag/exterior`) but, as far as checked, no splitting algebras;
- a simpler route to "some alpha works" when K is large: d is a nonzero
  polynomial of known degree, so among enough distinct elements of K one is
  not a root;
- small K: finite fields (primitive element of a finite field: cyclicity of
  F^* or a counting argument) and the stumble-to-idempotent bookkeeping;
- also needed: Bézout in K[X] (extended Euclid; `sag/acl-zmt/PolyGcd` has
  gcds over gcd domains, not Bézout), linear algebra over K (basis,
  kernels, minimal polynomials; not found in `unit`).

## Options

1. **Large residue field.**  Hypothesis: k has at least N distinct elements,
   N computed from [C : k] (covers k infinite and every residue field of
   characteristic 0).  Formalise the generic step only, via "a nonzero
   polynomial has a non-root among N elements".  Bounded: a few sessions.
2. **Full LQ 1.9** (all residue fields): option 1 plus finite fields and
   splitting.  Much larger.
3. **Keep the field case as a hypothesis** in LQ's form (product of
   monogenic étale factors, justified by LQ 1.9.2): the current state.

Independently, decisif Theorem 4.4 (layers 1-2) is a separate project:
it replaces "C is strictly finite and étale" by "E is étale over R".
