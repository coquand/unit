## PCC2 completion of the main results (2026-10-25)

**PCC2 Theorems 1 and 13 are proved and checked.** The scalar statement is
CFFR Proposition 8.3. Other developments can use the stable
`ExteriorPluckerSylvester.Over[Rg,setR].At[n,p,a,B].cramer` and
`.plucker-sylvester` interfaces. See `PCC2-READY.md` for the precise vector
conventions, import order, support APIs and remaining packaging scope.

The construction consists of the full perfect pairing (`ExteriorDuality`),
subset coordinates and products (`ExteriorCoordinates`, `ExteriorBasis`),
the higher adjugate and its first composition identity (`ExteriorAdjugate`),
vector contraction and transposition (`ExteriorVectorContraction`,
`ExteriorTranspose`), and degree/support and complementary-degree laws
(`ExteriorHomogeneous`). `ExteriorCramerIdentity` adds the second
composition identity, degree preservation, complementary-minor coefficients
and the all-subset Cramer formula.

`ExteriorReplacement` identifies those coefficients with determinants of
the actual column-replaced matrix. `ExteriorPluckerSylvester` restricts the
sum to subsets of cardinality p and proves Theorem 1. It constructs the
general interior product by dot-duality, proves its degree, uniqueness,
linearity in the first argument and volume formula, then transports
Theorem 1 by star to prove Theorem 13. Star and unstar are also packaged
as inverse maps on complementary homogeneous degrees.

The former outstanding second identity, replacement coefficient, degree
restriction and final contraction identity are thus discharged. A separate
arbitrary homogeneous-form isomorphism, general shuffle lemma and packaged
reverse implication are not additional premises of these proofs; their
export status is explained in `PCC2-READY.md`. Identification of the original
ordinary adjugate definition with the degree-one higher adjugate is not yet
a separate interface.

All new files have their informal proofs and unfolding specifications.

## 1. Principles

- **Universal properties first, stated with univalence.** Each object is
  characterised by a proposition saying a map is an equivalence of types.
  The concrete construction is one inhabitant; uniqueness comes from
  contractibility of the type of all objects with the property (structure
  identity, as in `chatelet/AlgSIP.seg`, `chatelet/ModuleSIP.seg`).
  Functoriality and multiplicativity then come from uniqueness of
  factorisations, not from recursion on the representation.
- **Conventions of the library**: no type classes; recursion only through
  eliminators (`PRIMREC.md`); every file < 10 s (the ten-second rule);
  lock a construction right after its characterising lemmas; the general
  statement first, the instance second. The model assumes univalence and
  function extensionality everywhere (`Axioms[l].UnivalenceLemmas`,
  `FunExtUA`).
- **Precedent for the style of statement**: `horrocks/Universal.seg`
  (`Hom(A[X],B) ≃ Hom(A,B) × B`, with `forward`, `backward`, `fb`, `bf`).

## 2. Universal properties

Fix a commutative ring R (set). Algebras are `chatelet/NCAlgebra.seg`
(`Over[R,setR].Algebra`: unital associative, not necessarily commutative,
set carrier). Write `AlgHom E A` for R-algebra maps.

**Alternating maps.** For an R-algebra A and n:

    Alt n A := (j : R^n → A, R-linear) × ((a : R^n) → j a · j a = 0)

(R^n as `Fin n → R`.) Consequence used everywhere: j a · j b = −j b · j a.

**Exterior algebra.** For E an algebra and i : Alt n E,

    IsExterior n E i := (A : Alg) → isEquiv (λ g. g ∘ i : AlgHom E A → Alt n A)

This is a proposition. The type

    Exterior n := (E : Alg) × (i : Alt n E) × IsExterior n E i

is contractible (centre: the model of §3; contraction: SIP for algebras
plus uniqueness of factorisations). So statements about "the" exterior
algebra transport along identities, and later representations (e.g. the
subset basis of cffr) can be added and identified with the model for free.

**Functoriality, from the universal property.** For f : R^n → R^m linear,
i_m ∘ f is alternating, so it factors uniquely as Λf ∘ i_n. Then
Λ(id) = id and Λ(f ∘ g) = Λf ∘ Λg by uniqueness. No recursion needed.

**Determinant, by the universal property of the top degree.** Let
`AltForm n` be the alternating n-linear forms (R^n)^n → R. Statement:

    evaluation at (e_1,…,e_n) : AltForm n ≃ R

equivalently: the type of normalised alternating n-linear forms
(D(e_1,…,e_n) = 1) is contractible. Its centre is det. In terms of Λ:
Λ^n(R^n) is free of rank one on e_1 ∧ … ∧ e_n, and

    a_1 ∧ … ∧ a_n = det(a_1,…,a_n) · (e_1 ∧ … ∧ e_n).

Then det(AB) = det A · det B (both sides are normalised alternating in the
columns of B after dividing by det A, or: Λ^n of a composite), and
det I = 1.

*As built.* No normalised forms: products of n generators have degree n
(`ExteriorDegree.seg`, a predicate by the Pascal recursion), and degree n
is free on ω, so i(b_1)⋯i(b_n) = det B · ω. With φ_A the algebra map
extending v ↦ i(Av), i(Ab_1)⋯i(Ab_n) = φ_A(det B · ω) = det B · i(a_1)⋯i(a_n),
using φ_A(ω) = φ_A(i(e_1)⋯i(e_n)) = i(a_1)⋯i(a_n).

## 3. The concrete model

Ungraded carrier, by recursion on n (large elimination is available:
`Fin` itself is `split { zero -> Empty ; suc n | r -> Plus r Top }`):

    Λ_0     = R
    Λ_{n+1} = Λ_n × Λ_n          (ω_0, ω_1) stands for ω_0 + e ∧ ω_1

e is the new basis vector, put in front. Grade involution α (an algebra
automorphism):

    α(ω_0, ω_1) = (α ω_0, −α ω_1)

Product (derived from e·e = 0 and ω e = e α(ω)):

    (a_0, a_1)(b_0, b_1) = (a_0 b_0, a_1 b_0 + α(a_0) b_1)

Unit (1, 0); +, −, scaling componentwise. Generators:

    i_{n+1}(a) = (i_n(a'), a_new · 1)      a = (a', a_new)

Check: i(a)² = (i(a')², a_new i(a') + α(i(a')) a_new) = (0, 0), using
α ∘ i = −i. Top coefficient: top_0 = id, top_{n+1}(ω_0, ω_1) = top_n ω_1;
det(a_1,…,a_n) := top(i(a_1) ⋯ i(a_n)). Smoke test before anything else:
for a concrete 2 × 2 matrix, det reduces to ad − bc by conversion.

*As built (`ExteriorModel.seg`).* The new basis vector is put at the
**back**: (ω_0, ω_1) stands for ω_0 + ω_1 ∧ e. With e at the front, top
would be the coefficient of e_n ∧ … ∧ e_1 and det would carry the sign
(−1)^{n(n−1)/2} (−(ad − bc) for n = 2). With e at the back, e ω = α(ω) e
gives the product

    (a_0, a_1)(b_0, b_1) = (a_0 b_0, a_0 b_1 + a_1 α(b_0))

and α, i, top are as above. Over an abstract ring the smoke test cannot
reduce literally to ad − bc: conversion gives (1·a)·d + (1·c)·(−b) plus
terms multiplied by 0 (from the unit and the zero components of the
generators). So the file states that normal form by `refl`
(`det2-normal`) and then `det2-formula : det2 a b c d = ad − bc` by the
ring laws. Cost: `det 4` of a generic matrix normalises instantly.

Grading, when needed: the degree-k part as a projection defined by the
same recursion (Λ^k_{n+1} = Λ^k_n × Λ^{k−1}_n, Pascal), not as the carrier.

**Interior product** (Laplace expansion, adjugate), recursively:

    v · (ω_0 + e ω_1) = v' · ω_0 + v_new ω_1 − e (v' · ω_1)

with the identity of cffr §5.1: a · (b ∧ ω) = (a · b) ω − b ∧ (a · ω).

## 4. Proving the universal property of the model

Induction on n. Given j : Alt (n+1) A, restrict to j' : Alt n A (first n
coordinates), let g' be its extension, and put

    g(ω_0, ω_1) = g'(ω_0) + j(e) · g'(ω_1).

(As built, with e at the back: g(ω_0, ω_1) = g'(ω_0) + g'(ω_1) · j(e), and
the graded commutation (ii) is carried in the induction for the
constructed extension, for every ε anticommuting with the image of j.
`Exterior n` is stated as ((E : Alg) × Alt n E) × IsExterior n E i.)
Needed lemmas: (i) j(e) anticommutes with every j(v) (from j(a+b)² = 0);
(ii) graded commutation j(e) · g'(ω) = g'(α ω) · j(e), by induction;
(iii) g is multiplicative, from (ii) and the product formula; (iv)
uniqueness: an algebra map out of Λ_{n+1} is determined by its values on
the generators, since (ω_0, ω_1) = ω_0 + e ω_1 with ω_0, ω_1 in the image
of Λ_n. This is where the real work is; everything in §2 follows.

## 5. Consequences, in order

1. Λ_n as an algebra; i; i(a)² = 0; α.
2. Universal property of (Λ_n, i) (§4); `Exterior n` contractible.
3. Functoriality; top degree free of rank one; det; det(AB), det I.
4. Interior product; Laplace; adj(A) A = A adj(A) = det A · I.
   *As built:* adj(A)_{ki} := det A[k ← e_i] (column k replaced by e_i).
   det A[k ← b] is linear in b, so (adj(A) A)_{kj} = det A[k ← a_j], which
   is det A for j = k and 0 otherwise (a product of generators with a
   repeated factor vanishes). This is the side used by the determinant
   trick (step 6). A adj(A) = det A · I and Laplace are left for when needed.
5. Naturality in the ring: for φ : R → S, det(φ A) = φ(det A) (by the
   universal property, or by induction on the recursive definition).
   *As built:* by induction on the recursive definition, through one
   generic lemma (`ExteriorRelation.seg`): a family of relations Rel k
   between rings R and S, closed under 0, +, −, with Rel 0 1 1 and
   Rel k a b, Rel 1 a' b' ⟹ Rel (k+1) (aa') (bb'), lifts componentwise to
   Λ_n, through α, the product by an element of degree 1, the generators,
   products of generators and top; so Rel 1 on the entries gives
   Rel n (det M) (det M'). Naturality is the graph of φ (`Naturality.seg`).
6. Determinant trick: over the commutative ring A[x] ⊆ L, the matrix
   xI − a with x m = a m gives χ_a(x) m = adj(xI − a)(xI − a) m = 0; with
   step 5 for A[X] → A[x], χ_a(x) = 0 when 1 ∈ L. Cayley–Hamilton.
   *As built:*
   - χ_a := det(X I − a) in A[X] is monic of degree n (`CharPoly.seg`):
     the second instance of the relation lemma, Rel k p c := "p has
     degree ≤ k and X^k-coefficient c" (A[X] → A). The entries
     X δ_ji − a_ji have degree ≤ 1 and leading coefficient δ_ji, so χ_a has
     degree ≤ n and leading coefficient det I = 1. Only products with a
     polynomial of degree ≤ 1 are needed, which is what makes this cheap.
     The coefficient lemmas on A[X] restate those of
     `horrocks/MonicReduction.seg` (without its division dependencies);
     a candidate for a shared file.
   - The trick in a commutative ring (`DeterminantTrick.seg`): M m = 0
     ⟹ det M · m_k = 0, from adj(M) M = det M · I and (AB)v = A(Bv).
     `Integral`: for ι : A → L, x ∈ L, x m = (ι a) m, evaluation
     A[X] → L at x (`Universal.seg`) and `det-nat` give χ_a(x) m_k = 0;
     with 1 = Σ c_k m_k, χ_a(x) = 0 (`integral`: χ_a bounded, monic,
     root).
   - Cayley–Hamilton needs a noncommutative target. `PolyEval.seg`:
     evaluation A[X] → E at t for any A-algebra E (Horner; t commutes
     with every value). `ModuleTrick.seg`: the trick for a commutative
     ring B acting on an abelian group V, Σ_j M_ji · m_j = 0 ⟹
     det M · m_k = 0. `CayleyHamilton.seg`: E as a right A[X]-module
     through evaluation at t; if m_i t = Σ_j m_j a_ji then m_k χ_a(t) = 0,
     and χ_a(t) = 0 when 1 = Σ e_k m_k. `MatrixCayleyHamilton.seg`: E =
     M_n(A) (rows; t r s = a s r), for each row c the matrix units E_ci,
     and 1 = Σ_c E_cc, so χ_a(a) = 0. Right multiplication is used so
     that the matrix of the relation is a itself, not aᵀ (det aᵀ = det a
     is not built).
7. Minors: Λ^k(A) has the k × k minors as coordinates; ideals Δ_k;
   rank-r projectors (Δ_{r+1} = 0); McCoy (cffr Thm 2.4–2.5).
8. Later: trace, norm, discriminant of a finite free algebra (Châtelet).

## 7. Risks and open points

- Recursion discipline (settled): every operation is a `split` on n with n
  first; the products `eprod n m` take n as a fixed argument and split on m.
- Conversion cost on nested pairs for larger n; the smoke test and the
  ten-second rule will show it early.
- Whether to state `Alt` for an arbitrary module M (via `Modules`) or only
  for R^n. Start with R^n; generalise if the minors or Châtelet need it.
