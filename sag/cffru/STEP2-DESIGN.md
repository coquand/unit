# Step 2: the canonical determinant line (design)

Source: Buchsbaum–Eisenbud, *Some structure theorems for finite free
resolutions* (BE1.pdf), Theorem 3.1 and the remark after it for projective
modules (p. 99–100), and §4 (linear algebra).  cffr §8 is Theorem 3.1 in
coordinates; the remark is the form without orientations, so without signs.
Goal (HANDOFF-CFFR-UNIVALENT.md, step 2): remove the Gaps entry "the dual
element of Lemma 8.4 is unstar(beta) = ±beta*, the Cayley determinant is the
paper's up to sign", and give Thm 8.7 a canonical line Det(F) → R.

## 1. The statement

BE's remark (P_k projective of rank s_k, r_k = rank f_k, r_k + r_(k+1) = s_k):

    R_n = ∧^(s_n) P_n ,   R_k = ∧^(s_k) P_k ⊗ R*_(k+1) ,
    unique a_k : R_k → ∧^(r_k) P_(k-1)  with  a_n = ∧^(r_n) f_n  and
    ∧^(r_k) f_k = a_k ∘ (1 ⊗ a*_(k+1)) ∘ n*,
    n : ∧^(s_k) P_k* ⊗ ∧^(r_(k+1)) P_k → ∧^(r_k) P_k*  (natural iso).

Every R_k is a line (finite free of rank 1), and for a line L, L ⊗ K* = Hom(K, L)
canonically.  Through that, n* becomes the wedge product.  So the statement
needs no tensor products and no duals:

    R_(n+1) = R ,  a_(n+1) = 1 : R → ∧^0 P_n = R ;
    R_k = Hom(R_(k+1), ∧^(s_k) P_k)          (a line) ;
    a_k : R_k → ∧^(r_k) P_(k-1) the unique linear map with
      ∧^(r_k) f_k (x) = a_k (ρ ↦ x ∧ a_(k+1)(ρ))   for x in ∧^(r_k) P_k.   (*)

For k = 1, P_0 = R and r_1 = 1: **Det(F) := R_1** and **a_1 : Det(F) → R**.
Thm 8.7 then reads: the image of a_1 is the gcd ideal of D_1(f_1).

The only pairing used is x ∧ y : ∧^(r_k) × ∧^(r_(k+1)) → ∧^(s_k), the
"canonical pairing" of the handoff.  The order x ∧ a(ρ) is a convention.  It
matches cffr: in coordinates, with β = a_(k+1)(1) and γ = a_k(the basis
element),

    top(x ∧ β) = dot(star x, β) = dot(x, unstar β)

(`ExteriorDuality.star-pair`, `ExteriorTranspose.star-isometry`).  So cffr's
B_K = bhat_K γ with bhat = unstar β (Lemma 8.4) **is** (*) in coordinates,
with no sign.  The (-1)^(pq) appears only when unstar β is compared with
the Hodge β*, and (*) never does.

## 2. Method: characterise, prove contractible on R^n, transport

Each basis-free object is defined as the centre of a type that is
contractible, or a proposition, for every finite free module:

1. state the characterising property P(M) for any module M;
2. prove isContr P(R^n) (or isProp plus an element) with exterior/ and cffr/;
3. isContr is a proposition and M is merely R^n (`FreeModules.FinFree`), so by
   structure identity (`ModuleSIP.sip`) isContr P(M) holds for every finite
   free M;
4. take the centre.  Nothing depends on a basis, and naturality under linear
   isomorphisms is transport.

This is the step-1 transport lemma applied to the definitions themselves,
not only to proofs.  It is also how exterior/ already treats ∧R^n:
`ExteriorContr` proves the type of exterior algebras on n generators
contractible.

## 6. Open points and risks

- **Levels.** `Ext M` lives in U l+ but its centre has its carrier in U l;
  the transport stays in U l+.  Replacement (S1) makes the type of finite
  free modules small, at the price of the full module SIP.
- **AltM vs Alt n.** (Free n).M is Vec n, but `Alternating`'s `vadd`/`vscale`
  must agree with `Products.Pow`'s `madd`/`sm` (pointwise in both: expected
  to be definitional).
- **E4 cost.** A basis-free degree as a generated submodule needs the
  comparison with `Deg` on R^n (products of k generators have degree k:
  `DG.deg-eprod`; the converse by `ExteriorBasis.basis-product`).
- **T1 basis-free.** BE prove exactness of (3.3) by localising at primes of
  depth < 2; cffr replaces this by Lemma 8.2 and Gr ≥ 2.  Uniqueness only
  needs regularity of I(a_(k+1)) (cffr: Gr ≥ 2, hence regular), and can be
  done in coordinates and transported (the statement is a proposition).
- **Lines as Hom.** Using Hom(K, L) instead of L ⊗ K* avoids tensor products
  (sag/tensor/ is SAG §3's tensor of modules and is not needed).  The price:
  R_k is defined recursively by Hom, which is how BE's R_k = ∧^(s_k)P_k ⊗ R*_(k+1)
  reads anyway.
- **Not covered by step 2.** BE Thm 3.1(b) (Rad I(a_k) = Rad I(f_k)), which
  uses primes; BE Cor 5.1 (products of the I(f_k)).  Candidates for later.
- **Basis-free D_k (step 1 leftover).** It becomes the image ideal of
  ∧^k φ against the coordinate forms of ∧^k N (BE2's rank via ∧^k φ ≠ 0):
  define it after E3–E5.
