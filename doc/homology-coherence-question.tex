\title[Homology and coherence]{Homology from universal properties:\\
 results and a coherence question}
\date{1 November 2026}
\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
We describe a checked development of the beginnings of homology in
ordinary dependent type theory with univalence and an axiomatic ambient
category of higher categories. Copowers provide the basic construction;
a representable stability assumption gives abelian groups,
Mayer--Vietoris exactness on pointed components and a suspension equivalence. Excision and the
subsequent results currently assume the pentagon for the chosen internal
composition. We explain the outstanding problem of deriving this
pentagon. A finite recursive construction of exact sequences of groups
from pointed fibres is now checked; its homology instance gives the
positive-degree Mayer--Vietoris sequence of abelian groups, exact at
all three repeating positions. Its degree-zero extension and
categorical naturality remain open.
We ask whether universal properties can supply a finite,
reusable treatment of the coherence required by structural arguments in
homology.
\end{abstract}
\maketitle

\section{Purpose and mathematical setting}

The aim is a formal language in which arguments using stable higher
categories and universal properties retain their usual mathematical
form. Homology is a test case: form the colimit of a constant diagram,
use stability, and extract homotopy groups. The following is a
mathematical account of the results already checked and of the remaining
question. Every proposition below is conditional on the hypotheses
specified; no construction of a model of all these hypotheses is claimed.

The foundations are ordinary dependent type theory with universes,
identity types and univalence. Function extensionality follows from
univalence. Image and replacement assumptions provide the propositional
truncations and set quotients used below. We write $\pi_0Y=\|Y\|_0$.
Pushouts of types, when needed, are specified by their mapping universal
property; no new type-theoretic rules are introduced.

Following Sattler and W\"arn's axiomatic approach~\cite{SW}, we are given
$\Cat:\U_{\ell+1}$, types $\Map(A,B):\U_\ell$, identities, composition,
and associativity and unit paths. In particular,
\[
 a_{F,G,K}:(F\circ G)\circ K=F\circ(G\circ K).
\]
The types $\Map(A,B)$ need not be sets. We assume specified pentagon and
triangle identities for these paths. These are equalities between paths,
not strict equations for composition.

The structural axioms give a terminal category $\pt$, pullbacks,
exponentials, and an interval $\I$ with two objects $0,1$. The interval
classifies monotone maps to the two-element order. The ambient category
is univalent; the internal categories satisfy a Rezk univalence axiom;
and the interval detects equivalences of categories. There is a Segal
axiom in dimension two. The simplices are constructed from the interval,
with $\Delta^0=\pt$ and $\Delta^{n+1}=\Fun(\Delta^n,\I)$.

For $C:\Cat$, set
\[
 \Ob C=\Map(\pt,C),\qquad
 \Hom_C(x,y)=\sum_{m:\Map(\I,C)}(m\circ0=x)\times(m\circ1=y).
\]
We distinguish ambient composition $F\circ G$ from internal composition:
for $f:x\to y$ and $g:y\to z$ write $\comp f g:x\to z$, in diagrammatic
order. A square in $C$ includes a specified path between its two composites.

\begin{proposition}[Segal and internal composition]
For every $n\geq0$, restriction to the spine is an equivalence
\[
 \Map(\Delta^n,C)\simeq
 \sum_{x_0,\ldots,x_n:\Ob C}\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\Hom_C(x_i,x_{i+1}).
\]
Consequently, the type of simplex fillers of each prescribed spine is
contractible. Internal composition, unit laws and a specified associator
\[
 \alpha_{f,g,h}:\comp{(\comp f g)}h=\comp f{(\comp g h)}
\]
have been constructed. Functors preserve identities and composition up to
specified paths. With the univalence and detection axioms, fully faithful
functors reflect invertibility and induce embeddings on object types;
fully faithful, essentially surjective functors are equivalences.
\end{proposition}

These results use the ambient pentagon and triangle, but no ambient
coherence above them. In particular, the quantifier over $n$ in the Segal
theorem does not require successively higher coherence assumptions.
The pentagon for the \emph{chosen internal associator} has not yet been
derived.

\section{Copowers and excision}

Fix $D:\Cat$. Let $\U_k$ be a universe strictly smaller than $\U_\ell$.
For $X:\U_k$ and $A:\Ob D$, assume an object $X\otimes A$ and arrows
$\eta_x:A\to X\otimes A$, such that, for every $T:\Ob D$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:copower}
 r_{X,A,T}:\Hom_D(X\otimes A,T)\xrightarrow{\ \simeq\ }
              (X\to\Hom_D(A,T)),\qquad
 g\longmapsto(x\longmapsto\comp{\eta_x}g).
\end{equation}
This is the mapping universal property of the colimit of the constant
$X$-diagram at $A$. The size restriction is part of the assumptions.

\begin{proposition}[Basic copower properties]
A map $f:X\to Y$ induces $t_A(f):X\otimes A\to Y\otimes A$, with
$\comp{\eta_x}{t_A(f)}=\eta_{f(x)}$. There are paths
\[
 t_A(\id)=\id,\qquad
 t_A(g\circ f)=\comp{t_A(f)}{t_A(g)}.
\]
Homotopic maps have equal images, and equivalences induce invertible
arrows. Moreover,
\[
 \pt\otimes A\simeq A,\qquad \varnothing\otimes A\text{ is initial},
\]
\[
 (X+Y)\otimes A\simeq(X\otimes A)\amalg(Y\otimes A),\qquad
 (X\times Y)\otimes A\simeq X\otimes(Y\otimes A).
\]
Here $\simeq$ between objects denotes equivalence in $D$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Argument]
Invert~\eqref{eq:copower} to construct the arrows. Their comparisons
follow by restricting along every $\eta_x$, using the inverse's
computation path, associativity and function extensionality. The
coproduct and Fubini assertions apply the same argument to families
indexed by $X+Y$ and $X\times Y$.
\end{proof}

The coproduct statement is binary. These comparisons have been proved
individually; they are not an assertion that an entire coherent
copower functor has already been constructed.

\begin{hypothesis}[Internal pentagon $\PD$]\label{hyp:pent}
For four composable arrows in $D$, the two usual composites of the
specified associator $\alpha$ from
$\comp{(\comp{(\comp f g)}h)}k$ to
$\comp f{(\comp g{(\comp h k)})}$ are equal:
\[
 \alpha_{f\star g,h,k}\cdot\alpha_{f,g,h\star k}
 =\bigl((\alpha_{f,g,h}\star k)\cdot\alpha_{f,g\star h,k}\bigr)
       \cdot(f\star\alpha_{g,h,k}).
\]
Whiskering of paths is denoted by the same symbol $\star$.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{proposition}[Excision, assuming $\PD$]\label{prop:excision}
A pushout square of small types
\[
 \begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large]
 C\ar[r,"i"]\ar[d,"j"']&B\ar[d,"u"]\\
 E\ar[r,"v"']&X
 \end{tikzcd}
\]
is carried by $t_A$ to a pushout square in $D$, with the specified
commutativity path induced by the square and the copower comparisons.
The pushout of types is required to have its universal property for
targets in $\U_\ell$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Argument]
For $T:\Ob D$, compare cocones from the image square into $T$ with
cocones from the original square into $\Hom_D(A,T)$.
Restriction~\eqref{eq:copower} gives equivalences on the two legs and
on their path data. The resulting comparison square commutes. The
verification on the path data uses $\PD$: it compares reassociations
of four internal arrows, including a generator $\eta_c$. The pushout
universal property for types then gives the required equivalence for $D$.
\end{proof}

\section{Groups, exactness and suspension}

For this section assume $\PD$ and the following additional data:
a zero object $0_D$; a chosen suspension $\Sigma B$, given by a pushout
of $0_D\leftarrow B\to0_D$; and an object $\sph\in D$. No monoidal
structure or generator property of $\sph$ is assumed.

The stability assumption is specifically the following representable
condition: for every pushout square in $D$ and every $S\in D$, applying
$\Hom_D(S,-)$ gives a pullback of types. We do not assume that all finite
colimits in $D$ exist. The results concern the pushouts provided above or
given in the hypotheses.

\begin{proposition}[Loop equivalences]
There are equivalences
\begin{align*}
 L_{B,T}:\Hom_D(\Sigma B,T)&\simeq\Omega(\Hom_D(B,T),0),\\
 \sigma_{B,T}:\Hom_D(B,T)&\simeq\Omega(\Hom_D(B,\Sigma T),0).
\end{align*}
The first follows from the suspension universal property; the second
uses stability. The first carries the zero arrow to the constant loop
and is natural under postcomposition, with the basepoint change supplied
by $\comp0f=0$.
\end{proposition}

The naturality statement for $L$ uses $\PD$. For $n\geq0$, define
\[
 H_n(X;A)=\pi_0\Hom_D(\Sigma^n\sph,X\otimes A).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Groups and positive-degree homomorphisms]
Each $H_n(X;A)$ has a specified abelian group structure. For $n\geq1$,
the maps $H_n(f;A)$ induced by $t_A(f)$ are homomorphisms for these
structures. Identity and composition laws for the underlying maps of
sets hold in every degree.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Argument]
In positive degrees transport the fundamental group structure along
$\pi_0 L$. In degree zero use $\pi_0\sigma$ instead. Stability supplies
the further deloopings needed for the Eckmann--Hilton argument, proving
commutativity in every degree. For positive-degree homomorphisms,
naturality of $L$ identifies postcomposition with the loop map of a
pointed map. Such a map preserves loop multiplication. Transport this
fact along $\pi_0 L$.
\end{proof}

This last argument needs no coherence relating the chosen naturality
paths of $L$ for two successive maps. The homomorphism assertion in
degree zero, and the comparison of the two available group structures
on positive-degree groups, remain unproved.

\subsection*{Mayer--Vietoris exactness on components}
For the square in Proposition~\ref{prop:excision}, a connecting map
\[
 \partial:H_{n+1}(X;A)\longrightarrow H_n(C;A)
\]
has been constructed. The following statements are proved on sets of
components, pointed by the zero arrows:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $u_*i_*=v_*j_*$. If $u_*b=v_*e$, then there merely exists $c$
 with $i_*c=b$ and $j_*c=e$.
 \item $i_*\partial=0$ and $j_*\partial=0$. If $i_*c=0=j_*c$, then
 there merely exists $z$ with $\partial z=c$.
 \item $\partial(u_*b-v_*e)=0$ for $b\in H_{n+1}(B;A)$ and
 $e\in H_{n+1}(E;A)$. Conversely, if $\partial z=0$, there merely
 exist such $b,e$ with $z=u_*b-v_*e$. This difference uses the specified
 group structure in degree $n+1$.
\end{enumerate}
Here ``merely exists'' is propositional existence. Indeed, stability
identifies $\Hom_D(S,C\otimes A)$ with the type of triples
$(b,e,p:u_*b=v_*e)$. The connecting map is obtained from triples
$(0,0,p)$, using $L$ to supply $p$.
The boundary preserves the zero class. These results establish the
three repeating exactness positions, but do not yet give a natural long
exact sequence of abelian groups: additivity of the chosen boundary,
the degree-zero homomorphisms and categorical naturality remain open.
Boundary naturality has been proved for maps of pointed pullback
diagrams of types with specified cospan homotopies; its application to
the categorical diagrams still needs the comparison data.

A structural construction, described in the next section, now gives a
replacement boundary with additivity and exactness on both sides proved
together in positive degrees. Comparison with the original formula is
not needed for that result and has not been proved.

\subsection*{Reduced groups and spheres}
Write $!:X\to\pt$, and define
\begin{align*}
 \Red(S,X;A)&=\fib_0\bigl(\Hom_D(S,X\otimes A)
              \xrightarrow{t_A(!)_*}\Hom_D(S,\pt\otimes A)\bigr),\\
 \red_n(X;A)&=\pi_0\Red(\Sigma^n\sph,X;A).
\end{align*}
For a suspension $SX$ specified by the pushout $\pt\leftarrow X\to\pt$,
the equivalence
\[
 \Red(\Sigma B,SX;A)\simeq\Red(B,X;A)
\]
has been proved. Hence $\red_{n+1}(SX;A)\simeq\red_n(X;A)$ as sets.
With chosen desuspensions in $D$, satisfying
$\Sigma(\Sigma^{-1}B)\simeq B$, the resulting sphere computations include
\[
 \red_{n+j}(S^n;A)\simeq\pi_0\Hom_D(\Sigma^j\sph,A)
 \quad(n,j\geq0),
\]
and
\[
 \red_k(S^{k+m};A)\simeq\pi_0\Hom_D(\Sigma^{-m}\sph,A)
 \quad(k,m\geq0).
\]
Here $S^0=S\varnothing$ and $S^{n+1}=SS^n$. These are bijections of
sets; compatibility with group structures is not yet proved.

The intended example is spectra, with $\sph$ the sphere spectrum and
$X\otimes A\simeq\Sigma^\infty X_+\wedge A$~\cite{HA}. Taking
$A=H\mathbb Z$ should recover ordinary integral homology. Neither this
model nor this comparison has been constructed in the development.
In particular, $\pt\otimes A\simeq A$ is a coefficient normalization,
not the dimension axiom asserting the vanishing of positive homology
of a point. Such vanishing requires further information about $A$.

\section{One fibre construction supplies an exact sequence}
\label{sec:fibres}

The following structural result has been checked using only the
type-theoretic foundations. Start with one pointed map
$f_0:(Y_0,y_0)\to(Y_{-1},y_{-1})$. Recursively define
\[
 Y_{r+1}=\fib_{y_{r-1}}(f_r),\qquad
 f_{r+1}:Y_{r+1}\longrightarrow Y_r
\]
by the fibre projection, with its canonical basepoint.

\begin{proposition}[Exact fibre tower]
For every $r\geq0$, the sequence
\[
 \pi_1Y_{r+1}\xrightarrow{(f_{r+1})_*}\pi_1Y_r
 \xrightarrow{(f_r)_*}\pi_1Y_{r-1}
\]
consists of group homomorphisms and is exact at $\pi_1Y_r$.
A specified commuting square of pointed initial maps, including its
basepoint compatibility, induces commuting squares of groups at every
stage.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Argument]
The equivalence
$\Omega\fib(f)\simeq\fib(\Omega f)$ is proved together with its
compatibility with the projection to $\Omega Y$. It therefore gives
both the null-composite and the lifting assertion for the same loop
maps. Effectiveness of set truncation gives exactness on components;
these maps are homomorphisms because they are loop maps. Iteration is
ordinary recursion on $\mathbb N$. A pointed square induces a square
of fibre inclusions. Its new basepoint compatibility follows from the
first-projection computation of the path of pairs; it is not additional
input. Applying this construction recursively proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

The second fibre is identified with $\Omega Y_{-1}$, including its
map into the first fibre. More generally, for a pointed homotopy
pullback $Q\simeq B\times_P E$, projection
$Q\to B\times E$ has fibre equivalent to $\Omega P$, over the boundary
map into $Q$. This gives one connecting homomorphism
$\pi_2P\to\pi_1Q$ whose image is the kernel of the product projection.
The map and its exactness come from the same fibre inclusion.

Apply this to the representable pullback supplied by stability for
the excision square, with probe $\Sigma^n\sph$. The pointed
suspension--loop equivalences identify the source and target groups
with the existing homology groups. The projection paths identify the
outgoing maps with the existing $i_*$ and $j_*$. The following stronger
statement is now checked.

\begin{proposition}[Positive-degree Mayer--Vietoris]
There are homomorphisms
$\partial^{\mathrm{fib}}:H_r(X;A)\to H_{r-1}(C;A)$ for every $r\geq2$.
For every such $r$, the sequence
\[
\begin{aligned}
 H_r(B;A)\times H_r(E;A)&\xrightarrow{u_*-v_*}H_r(X;A)
 \xrightarrow{\partial^{\mathrm{fib}}}H_{r-1}(C;A)\\
 &\xrightarrow{(i_*,j_*)}H_{r-1}(B;A)\times H_{r-1}(E;A)
 \xrightarrow{u_*-v_*}H_{r-1}(X;A)
\end{aligned}
\]
consists of homomorphisms of the specified abelian groups and is exact
at all three interior terms. The arrows agree as $r$ varies, giving
the positive-degree exact sequence ending at $H_1(X;A)$; exactness at
this last term is not asserted.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Argument]
For a family $V:Y\to\U$, inclusion of a fibre
$V(y)\to\sum_{x:Y}V(x)$ has fibre over $(y,v)$ equivalent to
$\Omega Y$: the equivalence sends a path of pairs to its projection
in $Y$. Applying this to the pullback cone identifies the fibre of
$\Omega P\to Q$ with $\Omega(B\times E)$. Its map to $\Omega P$ is
the difference of the two loop maps. Hence the same fibre-sequence
theorem gives exactness at $\pi_2P$, and looping the difference gives
the usual group difference on $\pi_2$.

One general lemma simplifies the compatibility argument. If loops at
$z:Z$ commute, conjugation by any loop at $z$ is the identity.
Thus transport of loops along two paths $x=z$ agrees. Homotopic maps
into $(Z,z)$ therefore induce equal loop maps with arbitrary choices
of pointing paths; no compatibility between those pointing paths
and the homotopy is required. Eckmann--Hilton applies this to loop
spaces. Together with the already proved naturality of $L$, it
identifies the maps on $\pi_2$ with the usual homology maps, without
an additional coherence law for $L$.

Exactness at $H_{r-1}(C;A)$ is the fibre inclusion theorem above.
Exactness at the product follows directly from the representable
pullback: $u_*b-v_*e=0$ is equivalent to $u_*b=v_*e$. Finally,
difference of homomorphisms into an abelian group is a homomorphism.
\end{proof}

No coherence assumption beyond $\PD$ has been introduced. These
results establish the conventional positive-degree sequence with one
boundary, without comparing it to the earlier direct formula.
Extending this sequence through degree zero, and establishing its
naturality for the intended maps of categorical or copower diagrams,
remain open. The recursive fibre tower itself already has naturality
for specified pointed input squares.

\section{Coherent uniqueness of universal witnesses}
\label{sec:witnesses}

There is a general, fully proved coherence result in ordinary type
theory. If $e:V\simeq W$ and $w:W$, the type
\[
 \sum_{v:V}(e(v)=w)
\]
is contractible. This includes the computation path as part of the
witness. Every iterated identity type with a specified globular boundary
in this fibre is also contractible.

This assertion has a finite formulation. For a type $Z$, define boundary
types $B_n$ and cell families $E_n:B_n\to\U$ recursively by
\[
 B_0=\pt,\quad E_0(*)=Z,\qquad
 B_{n+1}=\sum_{b:B_n}E_n(b)\times E_n(b),\quad
 E_{n+1}(b,p,q)=(p=q).
\]
Contractibility of $Z$ implies
\[
 \prod_{n:\mathbb N}\prod_{b:B_n}\isContr(E_n(b)).
\]
The proof is induction, using that identity types in a contractible type
are contractible. Dependent families of contractible choices likewise
have a contractible type of sections.

For copowers this applies to
\[
 W(h)=\sum_{g:\Hom_D(X\otimes A,T)}(r_{X,A,T}(g)=h).
\]
Identity and composition comparisons of universal witnesses have been
constructed in such fibres, retaining the computation paths. These
comparisons have not all been identified with the previously chosen
comparison paths after forgetting the witnesses.

Transport of witnesses along specified commuting squares is also
proved. Given $r:V\to W$, $s:V'\to W'$, maps $u:V\to V'$,
$v:W\to W'$, and $\theta_x:s(u(x))=v(r(x))$, transport is
\[
 (x,p)\longmapsto\bigl(u(x),\theta_x\cdot\ap_v(p)\bigr)
 :\fib_w(r)\longrightarrow\fib_{v(w)}(s).
\]
Identity and pasting laws follow from identity elimination. If $s$ is
an equivalence, the type of maps between these fixed fibres is
contractible, so their higher comparisons are available as well.
The outstanding issue is to supply the categorical boundary data
needed to apply this principle, compatibly with the specified operations.

\section{The internal pentagon: a precise outstanding step}

The contractible type of four-simplex fillers already exists. The
difficulty is identifying the chosen associators with the comparisons
read from its faces. For a simplex $T:\Delta^4\to C$ and face maps
$d:\Delta^3\to\Delta^4$, $d':\Delta^2\to\Delta^3$, reading an iterated
face involves comparing
\[
 (T\circ d)\circ d'\quad\text{and}\quad T\circ(d\circ d').
\]
These comparisons use the ambient associator, and compatibility among
them involves its higher coherence.

For clarity, call the ambient associativity and unit paths level~1,
and their pentagon and triangle level~2. A specified level~3 axiom
$\Kfive$ has been formulated: for five composable functors, the two
pastings of pentagon faces and associator naturality faces of the
three-dimensional associahedron $K_5$ are equal. This is an equality
between two equalities of paths.

\begin{question}[First coherence test]\label{q:pent}
Do the stated ambient axioms through level~2, together with $\Kfive$,
imply the pentagon for the particular internal associator constructed
from Segal fillers, for every $C:\Cat$?
\end{question}

A proposed proof first compares the action of three successive functors
on an internal morphism. For $F:C\to D$, $G:B\to C$, $K:A\to B$ and
$m:\I\to A$, the underlying morphism paths agree by the ambient
pentagon at $(F,G,K,m)$. Their endpoint comparisons are expected to agree
by $\Kfive$ at $(F,G,K,m,0)$ and $(F,G,K,m,1)$.
Both actual paths have now been expressed as paths in $\Hom$ over the
same underlying morphism path, leaving two explicit endpoint equations.
Their identification with the $K_5$ pastings remains open.

One must then pass to triangle boundaries and composition witnesses,
prove preservation of the chosen associator under functors, and apply
this to a four-simplex. Reduction of the internal pentagon to the
four-simplex spines has been proved; its value on those spines has not.
Thus Question~\ref{q:pent} is a proposed theorem, not a result already
available. All results above invoking $\PD$ still assume it independently.

Univalence does not by itself settle this point. It permits transport
along equivalences, whereas the face maps are generally not
equivalences. A universal characterization might allow coherence to be
transported, but the compatibility of the independently specified
ambient composition has to be justified.

\section{The open question for homology}

The broader objective is suggested by synthetic category
theory~\cite{CCNW}: check proofs at the level of universal properties,
stability and descent. For homology the desired argument is
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \text{copowers preserve pushouts}\\
 \Downarrow\quad\text{stability}\\
 \text{pullbacks of mapping spaces}\\
 \Downarrow\quad\text{homotopy groups}\\
 \text{exact sequences of homology groups}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
Each implication must include the relevant functoriality and
compatibility of the specified comparisons.

\begin{question}[A finite structural treatment of homology]
Can one give, within ordinary dependent type theory with univalence, a
finite collection of structural data and universal properties from
which the coherence needed for this argument follows uniformly?
More specifically, can such a presentation yield the copower
construction, its excision and suspension equivalences, and natural long
exact sequences of abelian groups, with compatibility of the universal
comparisons proved once rather than assumed separately as new
higher-dimensional laws?
\end{question}

``Finite'' here allows quantification over natural numbers and finite
diagrams, as in the contractibility theorem of Section~\ref{sec:witnesses}.
It asks for actual expressions and proofs in the existing type theory,
without judgmentally associative composition for $\Cat$ or new directed
type formers. Merely postulating that all the needed operations are
coherent would leave the problem unchanged.

There are two possible scales of answer. For homology alone, a fixed
finite amount of ambient coherence may suffice. Question~\ref{q:pent}
is the first unresolved test. Section~\ref{sec:fibres} now supplies a
finite recursive construction of exact sequences of groups, and its
positive-degree homology instance uses only the existing $\PD$.
There is presently no established bound for the
remaining homology statements. A stronger answer would characterize
ambient composition, or at least its action on simplex boundaries, by
universal data whose compatibility types are contractible. This would
explain systematically how coherent uniqueness of fillers becomes
coherence of the categorical constructions extracted from them.

Such a result should also admit an interpretation in the intended
higher-categorical models. For ordinary homology, one would additionally
need a suitable coefficient object and a comparison or normalization
theorem. More broadly, the same structural language should eventually
support arguments about stable categories and descent for families of
higher categories or stacks. Those extensions are motivations, not
results of the present development. The immediate question is whether
homology can supply a first substantial example in which the coherence
cost is absorbed into reusable mathematical principles.
