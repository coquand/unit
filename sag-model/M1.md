## 1. The cofibrations

Dually to finite non-empty posets P are the finitely presented
distributive lattices [1]^P with ⊥ ≠ ⊤. Three choices were discussed
(Coquand-Sattler, August 2023): (1) all decidable sieves; (2) the sieves
generated by constraints t = u in the lattice; (3) those generated by
t = 0 and t = 1. **We take (2).**

**Description.** A finite list of constraints on [1]^P is a surjection
[1]^P -> Q. Under the duality the (surjective, injective) factorisation of
lattices corresponds to (surjective on objects, injective and full) for
posets, so such a quotient is a *full subposet* A ⊆ P, and the sieve it
generates is the image of yA -> yP. A cofibration of P is therefore a
finite union of such sieves.

**Canonical form.** Every map f : W -> P factors as W -> im(f) ⊆ P, where
im(f) has the order induced from P. The map W -> im(f) is monotone, and
f factors through a full subposet A iff im(f) ⊆ A. Hence a cofibration is
the same as

    a down-closed family F of non-empty subsets of P,
    with  f lands in F  iff  im(f) ∈ F

(the union of the y(A), A ∈ F; conversely A ∈ F iff A ↪ P lands). So Φ P is
the set of abstract simplicial complexes with vertices in P. It is finite
data with decidable equality, so codes are compared by strict equality
(ruling seg-strict-in-V). All the operations of the core's base interface
`CBase.CB` are elementary in this form:

| Operation | Value |
|---|---|
| pre along g : Q -> P | {B ⊆ Q : g(B) ∈ F}, since im(g h) = g(im h) |
| empty, total | ∅; all non-empty subsets |
| end point e of [1] | {{e}} |
| union (`bigU`) | F ∪ F' |
| decided at the identity (`allS`) | P ∈ F |
| equaliser {f = g} of f, g : P -> [1] | the non-empty subsets of {x : f x = g x} |
| ∀ along I (`forallS`) | {A ⊆ P : [1] × A ∈ F} |
| dependent conjunction (`DConj`, for Swan Id) | {A ∈ F : A ∈ G(A ↪ P)}, for the family G given on F |

*∀ along I.* f : W -> P lands in ∀F iff (j, f g) lands in F for all
g : V -> W and j : V -> [1]. Taking the generic point V = [1] × W gives the
image [1] × im(f), and every other (j, f g) has image inside it. So f lands
iff [1] × im(f) ∈ F. This is the elementary form of the categorical
argument in the 2023 discussion (pushforward of full subcategories).

*Dependent conjunction.* If B ⊆ A ∈ F then G(B ↪ P) is the restriction of
G(A ↪ P) along B ⊆ A (naturality), so the family is down-closed. f lands in
it iff im(f) ∈ F and G(f) is total, and by naturality this holds iff
im(f) ∈ G(im(f) ↪ P).

*A second description (Coquand, same discussion).* F(X) is the free
suplattice on the semilattice of *embeddings*, the sieves generated by
maps f : Y -> X with f(a) ≤ f(b) iff a ≤ b. Such an f is injective, so up
to isomorphism it is a full subposet, and the semilattice is that of full
subposets under intersection. Freeness (e ≤ e_1 ∨ ... ∨ e_n iff e ≤ e_i for
some i) holds because the inclusion of A lands in a union iff A lies in one
of the pieces. This is exactly why distinct down-closed families give
distinct sieves. On the lattice side an embedding is a finite set of
constraints t ≤ u (t a conjunction, u a disjunction of atoms), and ∀ along
I removes a variable from them. This agrees with the table: ∀_t C is
C[t:=0] ∧ C[t:=1], and in a clause c ∧ t ≤ d or c ≤ d ∨ t one of the two
substitutions is trivial and the other deletes t. Example from that
message: for X = [1], the sieve generated by the discrete two-point poset
-> X (the maps constant on each connected component) is decidable but not a
cofibration. In canonical form it is not determined by images, since it
contains maps with image {0, 1} but not the identity.

## 4. Why not all decidable sieves

With (1), cavrep's construction fails. Counterexample (2026-11-09):

**Claim.** There is a finite poset X and a decidable sieve S on X that is
not generated by finitely many maps. Moreover S is the union of a strictly
increasing ω-chain of decidable sieves, so S is not a compact presheaf.

**X.** Five elements: a, b, e minimal, c, d maximal, and every minimal
element below every maximal one.

**Fences.** For n ≥ 1 let w_n be the word in X of length 4n+3 whose
letters in even positions are e, (a, b)^n, e, and whose letters in odd
positions alternate c, d. Let J_n be the fence x_0 < y_0 > x_1 < y_1 > ...
with one point per letter, and α_n : J_n -> X the labelling. It is
monotone, since x_i < y_i is sent to a minimal element below a maximal one.
Two letters at distance 2 in w_n are always different (e, a, b, a, ..., e
and c, d, c, d, ...), and e occurs only at the two ends.

**Lemma.** If β : J_n -> J_m and α_m β = α_n, then n = m.

*Proof.* β preserves labels, so it sends minimal points to minimal points
and maximal points to maximal points. A comparable pair in J_n has
different labels, so its image is a strictly comparable pair in J_m, that
is, two adjacent points of the fence. So β is a walk p_0, p_1, ... on the
path J_m that reads w_n, with steps ±1. If p_{i+1} = p_{i-1}, then
w_n[i+1] = w_m[p_{i+1}] = w_m[p_{i-1}] = w_n[i-1], against the remark
above. So the walk never turns back, and β maps J_n onto a segment of J_m,
forwards or backwards. The two ends of that segment are labelled e. In w_m
the letter e occurs only at the two ends, so the segment is all of J_m.
Hence the lengths agree and n = m. ∎

**The sieve.** Let S_N be the sieve generated by α_1, ..., α_N, and S the
union of the S_N. Then:
- S_N is decidable: f : W -> X is in S_N iff it factors through one of
  finitely many maps between finite posets, which is a finite search.
- S is decidable: if f = α_n β, then each connected component of W is sent
  onto a segment of J_n. A segment containing both ends is all of J_n, so
  n ≤ |W|. Every other segment of length at most |W| also occurs in w_m for
  every m ≥ |W| (the middle of w_m is periodic of period 4). Components
  are independent, so it is enough to test n ≤ |W| + 2.
- S is not finitely generated: finitely many generators lie in some S_N, and
  then α_{N+1} = α_n β with 1 ≤ n ≤ N, contradicting the Lemma.
- S is not compact: the identity of S = colim S_N does not factor through any
  S_N, since α_{N+1} is in S but not in S_N. The inclusions S_N ⊆ S_{N+1}
  are levelwise decidable monos.

**Consequence.** The core's Φ X (all decidable sieves) does not satisfy
cavrep Setting 40. Beyond the literal hypothesis, Construction 53 fails:
its endofunctor has a summand of partial elements over each φ ∈ Φ, and
that summand does not preserve colimits of ω-chains when φ = S. So
Cavallo-Coquand's replacement cannot be used as written on this base.

The sieve S of this counterexample is not of form (2): it contains α_1 but
not the inclusion of im(α_1).
